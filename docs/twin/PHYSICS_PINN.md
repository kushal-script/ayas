# Ayas physics: LED image-formation model and per-defect depth (PINN), 15 Nov 2026

Contract and results for `src/ayas/physics/`. Numbers come from `reports/physics_pinn.md`, which is regenerated by `python -m ayas.physics.experiments summarise`. Nothing in this work edits an existing file. Section 5 lists the proposed changes as diffs; none of them is applied.

**Why depth, and why only depth.**
- EN 10163-2 / ISO 7788 and IS 2062 cl. 7 grade dents and pits by **depth relative to plate thickness**. `configs/spec_steel.yaml` already holds those limits under `depth_targets`.
- The camera does not measure depth today. So every dent, pit or scratch above the minor size band goes to HOLD_FOR_QA with "depth not measured" (`modifiers.depth_not_measured`).
- A depth estimate from the LED frames, with an honest uncertainty, can decide those cases.
- The PINN is used there and nowhere else:
  - Blender already does the light transport, so the twin needs no learned lighting model.
  - Detection stays with YOLO.
  - A crack is always REJECT, so it needs no growth model.

## 1. Forward image-formation model (`optics.py`)

### 1.1 Equations and units

The frames are those of `ayas/twin/frames.py`:
- plate coordinates in mm: u to the right, v downward;
- world coordinates in metres: X = u/1000, Y = −v/1000, Z = w/1000;
- the plate face is the plane Z = 0;
- h(u, v) is the height in mm, positive out of the face.

```
n(u,v)    = normalize(−∂h/∂u, +∂h/∂v, 1)                     shading normal (textures.normal_from_height stencil)
Φ_i(x)    = P_i / (4π d_i²) · spot_i(cos a_i)                W/m², LED i (Blender "W", 683 lm/W), Cycles smoothstep spot
E_i(x,n)  = Φ_i · max(0, n·l_i)                              = lighting.light_irradiance_w (tested to 1e-9)
L_g(x)    = Σ_{i∈g} f(l_i, v; n, base, rough, metal) E_i c_i   +  Env_g(x, n)        W m⁻² sr⁻¹
S_g(x)    = k · L_g(x)                                       full-scale units; k = burst.exposure_k (fixed exposure)
```

- Each pixel's view vector v points from the plate point to the camera centre −Rᵀt.
- The camera integrates over a pixel, and `post.rectify` resamples bilinearly. Together these are modelled as a Gaussian point-spread function with σ = 0.035 mm, followed by sampling at the 0.08 mm grid. The depth estimator renders on a 0.04 mm lattice and average-pools to 0.08 mm. Crops wider than 48 px are block-averaged.

**BRDF f.** This approximates Blender's Principled BSDF, version 2 (Blender 4 and later; the pilot used 5.2.3).
- **Metallic lobe, weight `metallic`:**
  - GGX microfacet distribution with α = roughness²;
  - Cycles anisotropy: α_x = α / √(1 − 0.9a) and α_y = α √(1 − 0.9a), with the tangent at the plan grain + 90° (as in `render_scene.tangent`);
  - height-correlated Smith G2;
  - Schlick Fresnel with F0 = base colour (Specular Tint is white, so the F82 term is zero);
  - Cycles' multiscatter energy compensation, 1 + F_ms (1 − E(μ_v)) / E(μ_v), with E and E_avg tabulated by quadrature (`ggx_albedo_table`).
- **Dielectric lobe, weight 1 − metallic:**
  - the same GGX lobe with the exact dielectric Fresnel at IOR 1.5;
  - plus Lambert base/π, weighted by 1 − F(μ_v) (albedo scaling).
- **Two Blender facts that matter:**
  - The twin's relief is a **normal map**. It casts no shadows, has no displacement and gives no inter-reflection inside a dent. The model matches this by default; `shadows=True` adds a horizon test for real hardware.
  - Cycles' normal-map node applies `ensure_valid_specular_reflection`, which caps the shading-normal tilt at about 45° towards the camera. The twin therefore cannot show steeper walls, and `valid_reflection_normals` reproduces the cap.

### 1.2 The hood's own inter-reflection (found by the validation)

The ring sends its light at grazing incidence (8–12°). The flat plate mirrors that light onto the far hood wall, and the hood walls (albedo 0.03) are themselves lit by the far LEDs. On polished metal the plate then mirrors those dimly lit walls into the lens. In dark-field the direct signal on flat metal is tiny, so this second path is not negligible.

`hood_env` models it with one bounce:
- The hood interior comes from `lighting.HOOD` / `render_scene.hood_points`: the frustum, the collar and the lid.
- Each hood point is lit by every LED group directly, plus through the LEDs' mirror images in the plate.
- Its radiance is albedo·E/π, tabulated over the mirror directions r = 2(n·v)n − v of the crop centre (48 × 48 grid) and blurred by the lobe width (σ_r = 2α).
- The plate returns that light by the split-sum approximation: F(μ_v)·Env_g(r) + (1 − metallic)·base/π·E_hood.
- An integral of the plate's full BRDF over the plate area (`scatter=True`) gave the same maps within a few per cent, so the cheaper mirror-image form is the default.

### 1.3 Validation against Blender

Method:
- Pilot EXRs (4 test plates, Blender 5.2.3, 64 spp), rectified exactly as `post` does.
- The model is evaluated at the 0.04 mm texel grid with the **ground-truth maps** from `textures.render_maps` (height, albedo, roughness, metallic), then blurred and resampled.
- "Flat" means the plate interior more than 2 mm from the edges and away from every defect footprint, compared on 8 × 8 px bins.

**Open (handheld pods, no hood).** The direct model alone matches Blender.

## 2. Depth estimator (`depth_pinn.py`)

### 2.1 Model

For one crop around a detected dent or pit, the box is grown by max(0.8 mm, 0.45 × box size) on each side and kept inside the plate:

```
h_θ(u,v)  = D(x,y) · s_h · MLP_1(γ(x,y))          D = (1−x²)(1−y²): hard BC h = 0 on the crop border (x, y ∈ [−1, 1])
log m(u,v) = MLP_2(γ(x,y))                         a smooth material factor shared by every LED group
γ(x,y)    = [sin 2πBx, cos 2πBx],  B ~ N(0, σ²), σ = 1.2·(crop / defect size)/2 cycles per half-crop, 48 features
MLP       : 2 × 64 SiLU, last layer ≈ 0 (h starts flat), s_h = 0.3 mm
S_g       = k e^{a_g} [ m · L_g(n(h); base, rough, metal) + e^{e_g} Env_g(n(h)) ] + b_g
loss      = mean ρ((S_g − O_g)/σ_px) + 2e−4 |∇²h|² + 2 relu(h − 0.05 mm)²/0.05² + 0.05 (log m)² + 2e−3 |∇ log m|² + priors
σ_px²     = sensor variance of L − A (shot + read noise, from the frames) + (0.06 O)² + (4e−4 FS)²
ρ(r)      = log(1 + r²/2)  (Cauchy)
```

The learnable parameters per crop:
- base (log, prior from the material class with σ 0.35);
- roughness (logit, σ 0.6);
- metallic, for mill scale only;
- an exposure correction a_g per group (σ 0.1);
- an environment gain e_g per group (σ 0.5);
- an offset b_g ≥ 0 (σ 0.002 FS).

The class priors come from the DESIGN_STEEL B table. The grain direction comes from the whole plate image (`plate_grain_deg`, structure tensor). It is within 1–2° on brushed SS and aluminium, and 20° off on a cold-rolled plate with a large rust patch.

**Why the material field m(u,v) is needed.** It was found on the twin.
- In the dark-field tail, GGX gives D ≈ α²/sin⁴θ_h. Roughness and albedo therefore act as one per-pixel factor common to all groups, while the ratios between groups depend only on the normal.
- The twin burnishes dent floors: roughness −0.03 for ball dents and −0.05 for punch dents. That makes the floor 40 % darker for the same slope.
- With a uniform material the fit made the 0.627 mm ball dent 0.38 mm deep. With the true per-pixel maps it gave 0.616 mm.
- With m(u,v) the depth is carried by the L/R ratio and the boundary condition: 0.609 mm on the hood-albedo-0 render.

### 2.2 Optimisation

1. **Material stage, h = 0:** 80 Adam steps on the flat border ring.
2. **Joint stage:** 220 Adam steps, learning rate 4e−3 for the field and 1e−2 for the material.
3. **Optional L-BFGS** (`lbfgs_iters`; off by default, no gain measured).

Everything runs on the CPU with `torch.set_num_threads(threads)`.

### 2.3 Output and uncertainty

`DepthResult` holds:
- `max_depth_mm`: the deepest point below the crop-border plane, median over the ensemble;
- `depth_ci_mm`: the half-width of a ≈ 90 % interval (calibrated on the twin; see below);
- `volume_mm3`;
- `profile`: the u and v profiles through the deepest point;
- `residual`: the robust RMS of the normalised residual;
- `converged`: the depth varies by ≤ 3 % over the last 20 % of steps and the residual is ≤ 3σ;
- `runtime_s`, `members`, `rel_thickness`, `material_fit`, `notes`.

```
depth_ci_mm = sqrt( (1.645 · sd_ensemble)² + (SYS_REL · depth)² + SYS_ABS² ),   SYS_ABS = 0.02 mm
```

The ensemble uses n_seeds fits with different Fourier features; it shares the material stage. SYS_REL is the 90th percentile of the twin depth-coupon errors per class and number of LED groups (`depth_pinn.SYS_REL_TABLE`):

| Class | L/R | 3 sectors | 6 sectors |
|---|---|---|---|
| dents | 0.46 | 0.23 | 0.10 |
| pits | 2.15 | 1.12 | 1.06 |

- The ensemble spread alone is far too narrow: seeds agree to a few µm while the bias is tens of µm.
- Out of sample (the pilot plates, L/R): 21 / 21 hood dent estimates fall inside their interval, but only 62 % of pit estimates.
- Scratches have no calibrated interval yet.

### 2.4 Baselines

**(a) Classical.**
- The same crop, renderer (direct + env) and border material fit.
- Then per-pixel slopes (p, q), solved independently by robust least squares. With 3 or more groups a per-pixel material factor is solved too: classical photometric stereo.
- Then Poisson integration with h = 0 on the crop border (DST-I). This is the Frankot–Chellappa least-squares surface with a Dirichlet instead of a periodic boundary condition.

**(a′) No network (`method='grid'`).** The same loss on a free height lattice. On D3 it over-fits: 0.22 mm against 0.63 mm true, with a residual of 0.06σ. The network's smoothness prior is doing real work here.

**(b) No depth** is today's behaviour: HOLD_FOR_QA above the minor band.

## 3. Results on the twin (`reports/physics_pinn.md`)

Full tables and figures: `reports/physics_pinn.md` and `reports/physics_pinn_tables.md`. Everything is on the twin; nothing is measured on real metal yet.

**3.1 Depth coupons.**
- **Setup:** 6 materials × 16 dents and pits, hood, dark room. One Blender light group per ring LED; sensor noise added.
- **Dent depth error** (MAE, and share within 0.05 mm):

  | Method | L/R | 3 sectors | 6 sectors |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | PINN | 0.053 mm (62 %) | 0.034 mm (79 %) | 0.021 mm (92 %) |
  | Classical | 0.153 mm | 0.089 mm | 0.071 mm |

- **Median error relative to the EN 10163-2 Table 1 limit** for the plate thickness: 0.34 / 0.08 / 0.06 for the PINN.
- **Pits:** 0.15 / 0.11 / 0.11 mm, almost all of it under-estimation. Pits are not reliably measurable at 0.08 mm/px in this twin.
- **By material (L/R):** mill scale 0.012 mm; cold-rolled 0.027; ss 2B 0.056; brushed 0.066; galvanised 0.077; aluminium 0.077.

**3.2 Lighting invariance (pilot plates, real burst path, C0–C6, hood).**
- **Spread across conditions:** each dent's and scratch window's PINN depth varies by 0.005–0.023 mm (max − min); each pit's by 0.026–0.046 mm.
- **Against the truth:**

  | Dent | True | Estimated (median) |
  |---|---|---|
  | mill-scale punch dent | 0.353 mm | 0.353 mm [0.349–0.372] |
  | aluminium 6.9 mm ball dent | 0.163 mm | 0.126 mm |
  | aluminium 5.7 mm ball dent | 0.627 mm | 0.441 mm (−30 %, hood bounce, L/R) |

- **Pits:** 55–88 % low.
- **Open device (pods):** RETAKE in 4–6 of 7 conditions per plate. When accepted, the depth is biased (−34 % on the mill-scale dent). **Depth should run only with the hood.**

**3.3 Severity agreement.**
- **When the depth is used** (converged, calibrated ci ≤ 0.08 mm, interval inside one band): 11 / 30 / 48 of 96 coupon defects (L/R / 3 / 6 sectors), and 7 of 42 pilot hood captures. **All of them agree with the true-depth disposition, and none is more lenient.**
- **Example:** D5, a 6.9 mm dent only 0.16 mm deep, gets PASS in all 7 conditions. Today's size rule gives REJECT; the true-depth rule gives PASS.
- **Otherwise today's rule stands.** Today's rules are more lenient than the true-depth rule for 23 of 96 coupon defects (small but deep), and stricter for 39 (shallow).

**3.4 PINN vs classical.** The PINN wins in every configuration: 2.1–3.4× lower dent error. The classical method (per-pixel slopes + Poisson with the same boundary condition, the same physics and material fit) is not enough. With its own calibrated interval it would decide only 4–6 of 96 coupon defects.

**3.5 Runtime** (Mac M3, CPU, shared machine, load average 37–111):
- one dent with 2 seeds: 15–22 s on 1 thread, 12.5–27 s on 4 threads;
- one pit: 34 s;
- classical: 5–13 s.
- **Pi 5, projected (×3.5–4, not measured):** 1–1.5 min per dent, 2 min per pit.


## 4. Light directions: L/R or a sector depth burst

**Decision: L/R stays for the standard burst. Add an optional 6-channel depth burst** (B1, S0…S5, A, B2), taken only after a dent or pit is found with the hood fitted.

The evidence (`reports/physics_pinn.md` §3 and §5):
- **Paired on the same 96 coupon defects** (Blender per-LED renders, so the sector frames are rendered, not simulated):
  - dent MAE 0.053 → 0.034 → 0.021 mm;
  - share of depth-decided dispositions 11 → 31 → 50 %;
  - zero lenient decisions in every case.
- **The gain is on polished metal:** aluminium 0.077 → 0.032 mm; ss 2B 0.056 → 0.012 mm. Mill scale is already 0.012 mm with L/R.
- **Physics:** in the dark-field GGX tail, roughness and local albedo act as one per-pixel factor shared by all groups, so only ratios between groups carry the slope.
  - With L/R, one ratio gives the slope along the L–R axis. The perpendicular slope comes only from the boundary condition and integrability.
  - With 3 or more sectors the slope is over-determined, so material and slope separate.

**Hardware.** Six 3-LED modules on six MOSFET channels (four more GPIO). L/R, 3 sectors and 6 sectors are then software choices. Three sectors (two adjacent modules each, one extra channel) gives about half the gain.

**Cost.** Four extra frames, about 0.4 s at full resolution, only when needed.

## 6. Uniform-corrosion forecast (stretch, `corrosion.py`)

```
D(t) = r_corr · t^b             t ≤ 20 years   (ISO 9224:2012 Eq. 1)
D(t) = r_corr [20^b + b 20^(b−1) (t − 20)]   t > 20 years   (Eq. 3, upper estimate)
```

**Sources** (checked 15 Nov 2026):
- **b:** ISO 9224:2012 Table 2. B1 / B2 = 0.523 / 0.575 for carbon steel, 0.813 / 0.873 for zinc, 0.667 / 0.726 for copper, 0.728 / 0.807 for aluminium. Read from the ISO preview, `cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/53500/.../ISO-9224-2012.pdf`, Clauses 4, 5 and 7 and Tables 2–3.
  - B1 is the average from the ISO CORRAG regressions.
  - B2 adds two standard deviations, for an upper limit.
  - The tests check t^b against Table 3.
- **r_corr:** the ISO 9223:2012 Table 2 category bands, from J. Tidblad's ISO 9223–9226 classification slides (RRCAP/AIT). The zinc column also matches the American Galvanizers Association table.

**Output:** the years until the remaining thickness reaches a limit, given as fastest (upper rate, B2), typical (mid rate, B1) and slowest (lower rate, B1).
- Galvanised: the zinc coating is consumed first (85 µm by default; this is an assumption).
- Stainless: returns `None`, because ISO 9224 does not cover it.
- Every output carries the label: *uniform-corrosion forecast, advisory only, not pit growth*.

## 7. What runs on the Pi

- **numpy only** (always importable): `optics`, `severity`, `corrosion`, `device`, `baselines.poisson_dirichlet`.
- **torch, CPU:** `depth_pinn.estimate_depth` and `baselines.classical_depth`.
  - They need torch on aarch64. The app venv today has no torch; ExecuTorch is separate.
  - They run once per detected dent or pit in a hood capture, after detection.
  - Runtime: §3.5.
- **No image network is trained, and nothing needs JarvisLabs.** Each fit is inference-time optimisation on one crop.
- **Proposal, not built:** an amortised network to replace the per-crop fit, trained on JarvisLabs from twin renders. It would predict (h, m) from the L/R/sector crop plus the light geometry, and the PINN fit would refine it in about 20 steps. It is worth doing only if the per-crop time on the Pi is too long for the line (§3.5).

## 8. Limits (be honest)

**Renderer and twin.**
- **Normal-map relief.** The twin has no cast shadows, no inter-reflection inside dents and pits, and Cycles caps the normal tilt at about 45°.
  - Real low-angle light self-shadows dent and pit walls. `optics.horizon_shadow` (`shadows=True`) models it, but there is no Blender ground truth for it.
  - A displacement render is needed to validate it. That needs a copy of `render_scene` with displacement, which was not made.
- **The pits are the twin's darkened cones.** Real corrosion pits have undercut walls and corrosion product inside; photometry cannot see an undercut.

**Materials and the hood.**
- **Real BRDFs.** Principled v2 is an approximation. Mill scale (oxide-layer interference), brushed micro-grooves (anisotropy beyond GGX), oil and coatings were only covered as far as the twin covers them.
- **Grain direction** comes from the plate image, and was 20° off on a rust-dominated plate.
- **Hood bounce.** It is modelled with one bounce. The radial spread is wrong, and a floor of about 0.001 FS is unexplained. Both depend on the real hood's interior finish. **Recommendation:** a flocked interior, then re-measure.
- **Specular dark-field** on smooth metal leaves only 30–190 e⁻ on flat areas. Noise sets the floor there.

**Validation scope.**
- **Twin-only uncertainty.** SYS_REL is calibrated on twin coupons. Before any depth decides a disposition on the line, it must be re-calibrated on real gauged coupons: dents pressed into plates of each material, depth measured with a dial gauge.
- **Small samples.** 6 coupon plates (96 defects) and 4 pilot plates (10 depth targets × 14 rows). Material rows rest on 11 dents each.
- **Coupon runs** used one seed, the dark room and no hand shake. The pilot (all conditions, shake, ambient) used one seed.
- **Pi runtime** is projected, not measured. torch is not in the app venv.

**What this does not claim.** It does not claim certified depth measurement, which the advisor's banned phrases still forbid. The output is an *estimated* depth with an interval, and HOLD_FOR_QA remains whenever that interval is not narrow.
