# Aircraft-parts QC domain grounding for the Bharat AI-SoC PS5 handheld inspector: defects and how each is detected, public standards and limits, the Bengaluru industry context, test articles made from five 100x100x3 mm plates, public datasets, and what the team should and should not claim

(Research, 5 Nov 2026. Every finding carries its source; UNVERIFIED and (calc) are marked.)

## Summary

The aircraft framing is credible if the device is presented as a camera-based assistant for the shop-floor inspector, working on riveted aluminium details and sub-assemblies at Tier-2/3 suppliers. It should not be presented as an airworthiness or SRM damage-assessment tool. That boundary matters because PS5 is about manufacturing, and every public authority leaves acceptance to the OEM, the customer or a qualified inspector. FAA AC 43.13-1B is public and citable. Chapter 4 Section 4 covers metal repair: edge distance at least 2D and spacing at least 3D (4-57c), the next-size rivet for damaged holes (4-57b), deburring (4-58) and stop-drill holes of 3/32 or 1/8 in at crack ends (4-59a). Chapter 5 covers NDI: visual inspection makes up about 80% of all NDI, but Table 5-1 says it finds only surface discontinuities and generally only large ones. Chapter 6 covers corrosion: pitting, filiform, intergranular and exfoliation types (6-13 to 6-18), and corrosion removal with 20:1 blend-outs (6-115), with OEM limits or a DER for anything beyond that (6-113). AC 43.13-1B gives no numeric skin dent allowables; those sit in proprietary OEM SRMs. Piper Service Letter 1095A (2020, FAA-approved) is a rare fully public dent rule: depth about t/1.666, diameter no more than 2.0 in, at least 2x the fastener diameter from any fastener, and no cracks nearby. It makes a good labelled demo rule set. Public SRM-derived examples put corrosion blend-out at 10% of thickness on certain parts (Citation 560XL, Falcon 7X splice plate). The red dye kit is Type II visible penetrant, which ASTM E1417-21 §6.9.2 bars from final acceptance of aerospace products; the FAA (SAIB CE-18-26R1) and EASA advise against visible dye on safety-critical hardware. The 395 nm UV LEDs are outside the 347-382 nm excitation band defined in ASTM E3022 (my inference), so the device cannot claim fluorescent-penetrant compliance. Detection reality check: in the FAA/Sandia benchmark study (DOT/FAA/AR-96/65), 90%-detection crack lengths at rivets were 0.16-0.91 in across 12 airline inspectors, against about 0.10 in for eddy current. Cracks under 0.05 in were hidden under countersunk heads. Intergranular corrosion, cracks under installed rivets, elongated holes and composite delamination or porosity need eddy current, ultrasonic or tap testing, not a camera. For dispositions, Boeing's Q219P clause shows that a build-to-print supplier (the typical Bengaluru MSME) may only rework or scrap; use-as-is and repair go to the customer's MRB. AS9102 Rev C (June 2023) and AS9100D clause 8.7 frame the paperwork the assistant can automate. Bengaluru and Karnataka context is citable: HAL (A320 doors), Dynamatic (A320/A330 flap track beams), Aequs (Belagavi SEZ, over 5,000 components) and TASL (Jigani composites plant, Nov 2025). At VORTEX 2026 (Sep 2026), MoCA said MSMEs supply over US$4B a year and must invest in quality systems and skills, and Boeing reported about 300 Indian suppliers, a quarter of them MSMEs. I found no quantified, India-specific study of inspector shortage, so pain points should be worded carefully. Usable public data includes the IISc-Bengaluru Roboflow aircraft-skin set (CC BY 4.0) and DLR's 6,000-image specular-reflection dent set (MIT licence). AIRSD (8,091 images, part synthetic) has no stated licence. No public source shows synthetic-only aircraft-defect training transferring to real images, so the "digital twin" should be described as a CAD twin of each physical coupon used for augmentation, with all metrics reported on real images.

## Findings

- AC 43.13-1B structure (CHG 1): Chapter 4 'Metal Structures, Welding and Brazing'; Section 4 'Metal Repair Procedures' is paras 4-50 to 4-62. Chapter 5 'Nondestructive Inspection': Section 1 General, 2 Visual, 3 Eddy Current, 4 Magnetic Particle, 5 Penetrant, 6 Radiography, 7 Ultrasonic, 8 Tap Testing, 9-12 Acoustic Emission, Thermography, Holography, Shearography. Chapter 6 'Corrosion, Inspection and Protection': Section 2 Types of Corrosion (6-11 to 6-22), Section 5 Visual Corrosion Inspection Guide (6-63 onward), Sections 6-7 removal procedures and techniques (6-89 to 6-118), Section 8 Aluminum and Aluminum Alloys (6-132 to 6-136). (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2004.pdf ; https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2005.pdf ; https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2006.pdf (mirror of https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_43.13-1B_w-chg1.pdf, which returned 403 to the fetcher))
- AC 43.13-1B para 5-15: about 80% of all NDI is done by direct visual methods, covering cracks, corrosion, contamination and similar defects. Para 5-16: the basic kit is a flashlight, a mirror and a 2-10x magnifier. Table 5-1: visual inspection finds surface discontinuities only, generally only large ones, and scratches are easily misread. Penetrant finds surface-open defects only and needs clean surfaces with finishes removed. Eddy current finds surface and subsurface defects. Ultrasonic and X-ray find internal flaws. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2005.pdf)
- Composite awareness: AC 43.13-1B 5-105 says tap testing (coin or a hammer of 2 oz max) finds delamination and debonding, but only shallow defects in skins under 0.080 in, and it leaves no record. The ultrasonic section (5-89 onward) covers internal flaws such as pores and delaminations. So composite delamination and porosity cannot be detected with a camera. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2005.pdf)
- Corrosion types (AC 43.13-1B Ch 6 Sec 2). Pitting (6-13): white or grey powder, then pits that may run deep. Filiform (6-16): worm-like traces under paint, at 78-90% RH on slightly acidic surfaces. Intergranular (6-17): attacks grain boundaries; 2014 and 7075 are more susceptible when improperly heat-treated; it is subsurface. Exfoliation (6-18): surface grains lift; it is the visible evidence of intergranular corrosion, mostly on extrusions. Galvanic (6-19): corrosion builds up at dissimilar-metal joints (Fig 6-9 shows intergranular corrosion in 7075-T6 next to a steel fastener). Fretting (6-22): the 'smoking rivet'. Eddy current estimates corrosion metal loss against thickness standards (5-30). (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2006.pdf ; https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2005.pdf)
- AC 43.13-1B 4-57c: when the original layout is unknown, single-row rivets need edge distance of at least 2x and spacing of at least 3x the rivet diameter; double and triple rows follow Fig 4-5. 4-57b: an enlarged or deformed hole is drilled out for the next larger rivet only if the minimum edge distance and spacing are kept. 4-57f: blind rivets are barred from fluid-tight areas and heavily stressed locations. A loose friction-lock rivet shows the 'smoking rivet' pattern. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2004.pdf)
- AC 43.13-1B 4-52b: elongated holes or partly sheared rivets are checked by removing one or more rivets, so they cannot be seen with the rivets installed. 4-58: drill holes round and straight, deburr with an oversize drill or deburring tool, and do not overdrive rivets. 4-58b: 7075 is notch-sensitive, so avoid machine marks, nicks, dents, burrs, scratches and forming cracks. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2004.pdf)
- AC 43.13-1B 4-59a: drill holes of 3/32 in (or 1/8 in) at the extreme ends of a crack to stop it growing; this is a maintenance repair step. 4-59b: cracks come from stress concentrations such as nicks, scratches and tool marks combined with cyclic stress. 4-60: elongated bolt holes in fittings without bushings must not be reamed oversize; the fitting is replaced instead. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2004.pdf)
- AC 43.13-1B corrosion limits. 6-113a: corrosion beyond the aircraft or part manufacturer's damage limits means the part is replaced. 6-113b: if no manufacturer limits are available, a DER must be consulted. 6-115: dish-outs are elliptical with a 20:1 length-to-depth blend, and pits in critical areas are blended out. 6-136: final blends go to 400 grit, and the depth is checked against the rework limits. AC 43.13-1B itself publishes no percent-of-thickness limit. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2006.pdf)
- AC 43.13-1B gives no numeric dent allowables for metal skins; 4-50c says only that dented flanges or corrugations lose rigidity and must be restored. Numeric dent limits are in OEM documents. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2004.pdf)
- Piper Service Letter 1095A (24 Sep 2020, FAA-approved engineering, PA-28/32/34/44) gives a public dent classification. A 'negligible' dent is in skin only, within Table 1 depth (0.032 in skin: 0.019 in max; 0.063 in skin: 0.038 in; other thicknesses: about t/1.666), no more than 2.0 in across, at least 2x the fastener diameter from the nearest fastener, with no cracks, abrasions or creases within 2x the dent diameter and no deformation of interior structure. 'Minor' is the same except closer than 2x to a fastener. 'Major' exceeds those limits. Dispositions: negligible is acceptable as-is; minor needs replacement or added fasteners (at least 2D from the dent, 3D from existing fasteners, 2D from the edge); major needs replacement or an FAA-approved repair. (https://piperowner.org/talk/uploads/editor/hi/vuz8gqyvh3vs.pdf)
- Publicly reported SRM numbers: the Citation 560XL SRM allows blends up to 10% of material thickness and patches up to 2.00 in (Duncan Aviation, Oct 2014). On a Falcon 7X wing-to-winglet splice plate, corrosion blending is limited to 10% of parent material because the plate is not a primary structural element (Duncan Aviation, 2024). Study guides cite typical blend depths of 5-10% and taper ratios of 1:20 to 1:50, but every limit is part- and location-specific. (https://duncanaviation.aero/intelligence/citation-revisions-to-srm-allowing-typical-repairs ; https://duncanaviation.aero/intelligence/2014/July/falcon-7x-wing-to-winglet-splice-plate-corrosion ; https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/easa-part66-module-06/corrosion/corrosion-detection-removal-protection)
- SRM organisation that can be described publicly (secondary sources): Ch 51 structures-general and standard practices, 52 doors, 53 fuselage, 54 nacelles/pylons, 55 stabilizers, 56 windows, 57 wings. Page blocks are typically 1-99 identification, 101-199 allowable damage and 201+ repairs. Dent allowables depend on geometry and on distance to frames, stringers and fasteners. The SRM text itself is OEM-proprietary. (https://www.scribd.com/document/691845716/INTRO-TO-SRM ; https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/a320-structure-trainingpptx/251759505 (secondary, medium confidence))
- FAA/Sandia AANC visual inspection benchmark (DOT/FAA/AR-96/65, Sep 1996): 12 airline inspectors looked for cracks from under rivet heads on lap-splice panels. Their 90%-detection crack lengths were 0.16-0.36 in for 11 of them and 0.91 in for the twelfth, with a median of 0.258 in. Cracks shorter than 0.05 in were hidden under the countersunk head. Eddy current on the same specimens reached 90% detection at about 0.100 in. All inspectors said their calls needed eddy-current or other NDI confirmation, and the report warns that only large differences between inspector groups can be detected with modest sample sizes. (https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/12923/dot_12923_DS1.pdf)
- Fatigue cracks at fastener holes are the classic aerostructure failure. The NTSB found that Aloha 243 (1988) failed because maintenance did not detect disbonding and fatigue damage at lap joint S-10L, where multiple-site cracks grew from countersunk rivet holes. (https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916093758/http://www.ntsb.gov/publictn/1989/AAR8903.htm)
- An EDM notch is not a crack. An FAA/CNDE presentation reports that a 0.1 in EDM notch gave eddy-current signals equivalent to natural cracks of about 0.11-0.24 in, depending on the technique. Commercial reference standards use notches of 0.008-0.040 in, and the Boeing #369 fastener-hole standard has a notch 0.100 in from the countersink edge. (https://www.cnde.iastate.edu/mapod/2005%20February/MODEL%20ASSISTED%20POD_Swindell.pdf ; https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/probes/eddy-current/standards ; https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc619278/m2/1/high_res_d/142537.pdf)
- Penetrant standards named in AC 43.13-1B 5-64 and Table 5-6: ASTM E1417, MIL-I-25135E (materials), MIL-STD-6866 (process), ASTM E165, ASTM E1220 (visible, solvent-removable) and AMS 2647. Para 5-63j: each indication must be evaluated against acceptable limits. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2005.pdf)
- ASTM E1417-21 (approved 1 Sep 2021) para 6.9.2: Type II (visible or colour-contrast) penetrant may not be used for final acceptance of aerospace products, nor at any point on a surface that will also get Type I fluorescent inspection. In-process use is allowed where later fabrication removes the inspected surface. Reported by NZ CAA CAN 02-005, 17 Feb 2022. (https://www.aviation.govt.nz/aircraft/aircraft-maintenance/continuing-airworthiness-notices/show/02-005/)
- FAA SAIB CE-18-26R1 (30 Oct 2018, information only) says visible dye under white light should not be used on safety-critical engine, propeller or APU hardware, because its residue can mask cracks in later fluorescent inspection. EASA SIB 2018-17R1 endorses it, and CASA Australia has a similar bulletin. (https://ad.easa.europa.eu/blob/EASA_SIB_2018_17_R1.pdf/SIB_2018-17R1_1 ; https://www.casa.gov.au/index.php/aircraft/airworthiness/airworthiness-bulletins/use-visible-red-dye-liquid-penetrant-inspection-safety-critical-components)
- ASTM E3022 (LED UV-A lamps for fluorescent penetrant and magnetic-particle testing) defines excitation irradiance over 347-382 nm. The team's 395 nm LEDs are therefore outside the band an E1417-compliant fluorescent penetrant inspection relies on (my inference). The 1000 uW/cm2 and 20 lux limits are UNVERIFIED. (https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/100636/1cbcf56051c641118520407ad24f7bc0/ASTM-E3022-18.pdf ; https://store.astm.org/e3022-26a.html)
- Inspector qualification. AC 43.13-1B 5-4 says NDI personnel must be qualified and certified to FAA or industry standards (it cites MIL-STD-410 and ATA Spec 105). NAS 410 levels are Trainee, Level 1 Limited, Level 1, Level 2, Level 3, Instructor and Auditor, and the current NAS 410 merges the NAS 410 and EN 4179 requirements. EN 4179:2026 (published 16 Apr 2026) replaced EN 4179:2021, and existing certifications stay valid until they expire. A Tri-C page lists visual (VT) among NAS 410 methods; the VT training hours are UNVERIFIED. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2005.pdf ; https://standards.globalspec.com/std/978566/nas410 ; https://genorma.com/en/standards/en-4179-2026 ; https://www.tri-c.edu/workforce/advanced-manufacturing-and-engineering/nondestructive-testing-ndt.html)
- AS9102 Rev C (released 23 Jun 2023): Form 1 covers part number accountability (part, drawing revision, and a now-mandatory reason for full or partial FAI). Form 2 covers product accountability (materials, special processes, functional tests), and Form 3 covers characteristic accountability (a measured result for every ballooned characteristic, plus nonconformances). Rev C removed the Form 2 and 3 signatures. A design, process, tooling or location change that affects fit, form or function triggers a delta FAI; production-lapse triggers are set by the customer. (https://www.net-inspect.com/blog/as9102-rev-b-vs-rev-c/ ; https://gndctl.com/insights-library/how-to-fill-out-as9102-forms)
- AS9100D clause 8.7 (secondary sources): a use-as-is or repair disposition that departs from contract requirements needs customer authorisation; rework restores full conformance, while repair restores function without full conformance. AS9100D is still current. The successor IA9100 is expected in late 2026 or early 2027, according to consultancy sources. (https://training.mts.com/PCB%20Training%20Docs/LMS%20Training%20Files/PCB%20-%20Quality/QAM%208.7%20%20A%20%20Control%20of%20nonconforming%20outputs.doc ; https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/asq-cqi/ch9/nonconforming-material-mrb ; https://www.smithers.com/resources/2026/march/as9100-d-explained-and-what-comes-next)

Suppliers of Buyer-designed (build-to-print) goods may only perform 'Rework' and 'Scrap'; 'Use-As-Is' and 'Repair' go to Boeing's MRB. Suppliers may not perform MRB on critical, process-sensitive or flight-safety parts. (https://www.boeingsuppliers.com/content/dam/boeing/boeingsuppliers/boeing-suppliers/becoming/terms/clause-index/quality-clauses/q200-q299/q219p_04142017.pdf)
- AS9146 (FOD prevention program requirements) was issued Apr 2017 and reaffirmed Sep 2022. It covers program management, area designation, training and product protection, scaled to a FOD risk assessment, and complements the FOD provisions in AS9100 clauses 8.1 and 8.5. (https://saemobilus.sae.org/standards/as9146-foreign-object-damage-fod-prevention-program-requirements-aviation-space-defense-organizations ; https://ma.iaqg.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/9146-guidance-materials-2016.pdf)
- Alloy identification in AC 43.13-1B 4-23b: immersing a sample in 10% caustic soda turns copper-bearing alloys (2014, 2017, 2024) black. No blackening does NOT separate 1100, 6061 or 7075. Table 4-5 Brinell numbers: 1100-O 23, 1100-H18 44, 5052-H36 73, 6061-T6 95, 2024-T4 120, 7075-T6 135. Rivet code (4-57): MS20470AD3-5 is a universal head, 2117-T4 'AD' alloy (dimpled head), 3/32 in diameter and 5/16 in length; the diameter is given in 32nds of an inch. (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2004.pdf)
- Typical skin and rivet geometry. AC 43.13-1B 4-58g works an example on 0.032 in clad 2024 sheet: rivet diameter about 3x thickness, giving 1/8 in 2117-T4 rivets. Piper SL 1095A's skin table runs 0.012-0.063 in (0.3-1.6 mm), so 3 mm plates are 2-4x thicker than light-aircraft skins (calc). Secondary sources give a common pitch of 4-6D, transverse row spacing of about 75% of pitch but at least 2.5D, and edge distance of at least 2D (2.5D for flush heads). (https://raanz.org.nz/techproject/FAA%20AC43.13%20b/Chapter%2004.pdf ; https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14315/Figure-4-44-Rivet-Spacing-And-Edge-Distance-142.html ; https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/faa-amt-airframe/metallic-structures-sheet-metal/rivet-layout-driving-standards)

- (calc) Assuming the IMX500 pixel pitch is about 1.55 um (6.287 mm / 4056 px) and a 4.74 mm lens at 200 mm, magnification is about 0.0243. That gives about 64 um/px and a field of view of about 259 x 194 mm. A 4 mm crack (the low end of the FAA inspectors' 90%-detection range) spans about 63 px in length, but a tight fatigue-crack opening is sub-pixel, so the camera sees it only through a shadow or glint or through penetrant bleed-out. Dent depth cannot be measured from one 2D image without calibrated raking light or 3D. ((calc) from team-supplied IMX500 specs; FAA crack lengths from https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/12923/dot_12923_DS1.pdf)

## Recommendations

- Frame the project for PS5 as a 'handheld AI visual-inspection assistant for riveted aluminium aerostructure details at Tier-2/3 suppliers': in-process and final visual checks for handling damage, riveting and hole defects, corrosion, and crack-like indications, with automatic documentation. Use the in-service sources (AC 43.13-1B, Piper SL 1095A) only as public, cited references for severity heuristics, labelled as such. Do not present it as SRM-based damage assessment of in-service aircraft.

## Risks

- Credibility or disqualification risk from overclaiming. Words like 'airworthy', 'certified', 'approves parts', 'replaces inspector' or 'SRM-compliant' conflict with how aerospace QC works: acceptance criteria come from the customer drawing or specification, dispositions from the customer MRB, and NDI by certified personnel.
- Mixing in-service maintenance framing (skins, SRM, AC 43.13) with the PS5 manufacturing brief could confuse judges unless the report states clearly that AC 43.13 and Piper SL 1095A are used only as public severity heuristics.
- The red dye kit is Type II, which ASTM E1417-21 §6.9.2 bars from aerospace final acceptance, and the 395 nm UV pods are outside the E3022 band, so neither can be called aerospace-compliant penetrant inspection.
- Unknown plate alloy: 1100-O (Brinell 23) dents easily but tears rather than forming crisp fatigue cracks, and it does not show the intergranular or exfoliation corrosion typical of 2024/7075. 3 mm plates are 2-4x thicker than typical light-aircraft skins, so describe them as 'aircraft-representative coupons' only.
- Saw slits and EDM-style notches are not cracks. FAA/CNDE data shows that notch and natural-crack responses differ, so a model trained on slits may miss tight fatigue cracks; validate on real fatigue cracks separately.
- Physics limits at 64 um/px (calc): tight crack openings are sub-pixel, cracks under countersunk heads (under 0.05 in) are invisible, dent depth is not observable from a single 2D image without calibrated raking light or 3D, and elongated holes under installed rivets cannot be seen.
- No public evidence of synthetic-only transfer: zeroG's results are simulation-only, AIRSD has no ablation, and the DLR results are mixed real and synthetic. A 'digital twin like NVIDIA' claim without a real-image test set would be challenged. Isaac Sim or Omniverse also need NVIDIA GPUs, and the team only has an M3 Mac or rented cloud, with 18 days left until 23 Nov 2026.
- Dataset licensing and domain gap: AIRSD has no stated licence, Aircraft_Fuselage_DET2023 needs a subscription, Roboflow counts and licences could not be re-verified because the pages returned 403, and public sets show painted full-size aircraft at metres rather than bare coupons at 200 mm.
- Using proprietary OEM SRM excerpts circulating on Scribd or SlideShare would create IP problems; the team is responsible for the IP of all data and models it uses.
- Safety: 10% caustic soda causes burns and gives off hydrogen with aluminium; penetrant and developer aerosols are flammable; salt and acid corrosion cycling must be kept away from the Pi, power bank and LEDs.
- Naming HAL, TASL, Dynamatic or Aequs could be read as implying partnership or endorsement. The Karnataka market-share figures are state government claims, and the India inspector-shortage evidence is qualitative.
- Standards churn: AS9100D may be superseded by IA9100 in late 2026 or early 2027, and EN 4179:2026 replaced the 2021 edition, so cite edition and year explicitly. The ASTM E1417 and E3022 numeric limits beyond those quoted are UNVERIFIED.
- Small sample size (about 50 physical defects) gives wide Wilson intervals, and per the FAA AR-96/65 caveat it cannot support claims of equal or better performance than human inspectors.
