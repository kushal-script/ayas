# Making the handheld steel/metal-surface inspector (Pi 5 + AI Camera IMX500 + white LED pods, about 200 mm working distance) robust to any ambient lighting

(Research, 14 Nov 2026. Every finding carries its source; UNVERIFIED and (calc) are marked.)

## Summary

DECISION: Get lighting robustness from how the images are captured, not from the neural network. The stack, in order of importance: (1) The device's own LEDs at a fixed ExposureTime, AnalogueGain and ColourGains, with AE and AWB off. (2) An LED-off ambient frame, subtracted in LINEAR RAW. (3) Exposure = 10 ms, one full 100 Hz flicker period on Indian 50 Hz mains, with the frame period locked at 40 ms. (4) Separate left-pod and right-pod frames, combined with a pixelwise minimum to remove the LED's mirror highlights. (5) A quality gate that refuses captures it cannot handle and tells the operator why. (6) An optional black clip-on hood/standoff, the only real fix for direct sun. Augmentation is the third line of defence, not the first.
KEY NUMBERS (calc): the six owned 0.72 W, ~110 lm 3-LED modules (vendor figure) at about 212 mm give about 2.4-4.4 klux at the plate centre. Ambient:LED ratio r is then ~0.1-0.2 in an office (320-500 lux), ~0.2-0.4 on an overcast day (1 klux), ~2-10 in full daylight (10-25 klux) and ~7-40 in direct sun (32-100 klux).
After subtraction, per-pixel shot-noise SNR (9,610 e- full well, LED filling 75% of full scale) is about 59 at r=0.3, 35 at r=1, 22 at r=2, 6 at r=10 and 2 at r=25 (calc). So any indoor lighting is fine, and direct sun cannot work without shading. The device must detect this and say so.
Short exposure does NOT raise the LED:ambient ratio for continuously-on LEDs. Both signals scale with exposure time. Only strobed or overdriven LEDs gain from it. So choose 10 ms for flicker immunity, and dim the LEDs (PWM at 20 kHz or more, or fewer modules) if bright plates saturate.
ROLLING SHUTTER: the IMX500 is rolling-shutter. With 10 ms exposure and readout of up to ~33 ms, there is no clean gap between frames at 25 fps (calc). After every GPIO switch, use picamera2 capture_request(flush=True). A 5-state burst B1-L-A-R-B2 then takes about 0.4 s (calc).
RAW, NOT ISP OUTPUT: the IMX500 tuning file has a non-linear gamma, adaptive contrast (ce_enable=1) and temporal denoise. Each of these breaks frame subtraction. Use the Pi 5 unpacked raw stream (bit-exact) for the maths.
MOTION: about 2°/s of hand shake moves the image ~2 px per 40 ms frame and ~17 px over 0.32 s (binned mode, calc). Bracket the burst with two both-on frames, estimate translation with ECC/phase correlation, and reject anything above ~3 px. Strongly prefer a standoff that rests on the part: it also fixes the ±10 mm depth of field (calc), the scale and the ambient light.
GLARE: on a flat plate the LED mirror highlights land about 35 mm either side of centre (calc), so they fall on a 100 mm plate. Left and right highlights sit on opposite sides, so min(L,R) removes both (the moving-specular principle of Feris et al. 2004). Cross-polarisation needs film on BOTH the LEDs and the lens. With genuine Edmund film it costs Rs 1,791 per 50 mm piece, passes only ~15% of the diffuse light (2.75 stops) and makes the LED:ambient ratio 2.6x worse (calc). Treat it as Phase 2.
NORMALISATION: deterministic only. Flat-field from a white-card calibration, percentile scaling, and fixed white balance and gamma. No grey-world AWB, because red dye breaks it. No Retinex or homomorphic filtering on the device. CLAHE only as a reported ablation: no clean evidence was found that it helps CNN steel-defect detection.
PROOF: a 6-7 condition lighting test on the same held-out plates. Record lux three ways: a phone app (±60-113% error, so use it only for order-of-magnitude bins), the camera's Lux metadata on the ambient frame, and the logged r. Report P/R/F1 per condition with Wilson confidence intervals, plus SSIM against the dark-room image. Add a naive-ISP baseline arm to show what the design buys. Pass = F1 drop of 0.05 or less versus the dark room, and zero silent failures.
HONEST LIMITS: direct sun without the hood; mirror-polished parts where the left and right highlights overlap; moving shadows or odd-frequency LED flicker during the 0.4 s burst; very dark mill scale. The existing plan (<repo>/docs/Phase1_build_plan.md, lines ~406-412: SRGGB10 raw, AE/AWB off, 10/20/30 ms exposures, dark frames) already matches this. The handheld version adds the B1/B2 motion check, the L/R min, and the gate.

## Findings

- Raspberry Pi AI Camera (IMX500) video modes are only 2028x1520p30 (2x2 binned) and 4056x3040p10. f = 4.74 mm, F1.79, focus approx 20 cm to infinity, no interchangeable lens mount. (https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html)
- AI Camera retailer spec: 2x2 binned 2028x1520 10-bit 30 fps; full 4056x3040 10-bit 10 fps; IR cut filter integrated; HFOV 66°, VFOV 52.3°, diagonal 78.3°; 1.55 µm pixels. (https://www.waveshare.com/Raspberry-Pi-AI-Camera.htm)
- The IMX500 is a rolling-shutter sensor according to third-party module and camera makers. Sony's press release does not state the shutter type. (https://www.edmundoptics.com/p/lucid-vision-labs-sensaiz-szp123s-001-sony-imx500-1233mp-color-camera/50018/ ; https://download.kamami.pl/p1204703-Arducam_B0566_IMX500_USB3_UVC_Camera_Product_Brief.pdf)
- IMX500 saturation signal (minimum) is approx 9,610 e-; unit cell 1.55 µm. (https://sony.mediaroom.com/2020-05-14-Sony-to-Release-Worlds-First-Intelligent-Vision-Sensors-with-AI-Processing-Functionality)
- Picamera2: AeEnable=False stops automatic gain/exposure updates. AwbEnable=False stops colour-gain updates. Setting ColourGains (red, blue) disables AWB. FrameDurationLimits (e.g. (40000, 40000)) fixes the frame period. The FrameDuration metadata reports the actual interval. (https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/652-raspberry-pi-camera-module-2/documents/RP-008156-DS-7-picamera2-manual.pdf)
- Picamera2: controls set after the camera has started take several frames to apply, notably ExposureTime and AnalogueGain. Verify the applied values in the frame metadata. (https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/652-raspberry-pi-camera-module-2/documents/RP-008156-DS-7-picamera2-manual.pdf (sec. 5.1.3))
- Picamera2 sec. 6.4.1 addresses exactly the case of 'a light being turned on or off'. capture_request(flush=True) guarantees the first pixel began exposing no earlier than the call. SensorTimestamp is sampled at start-of-frame, when the first pixel is read out, so exposure start = timestamp minus exposure time. (https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/652-raspberry-pi-camera-module-2/documents/RP-008156-DS-7-picamera2-manual.pdf)
- On Pi 5, requesting an unpacked raw format returns uncompressed 16-bit, left-shifted pixels that are a bit-exact copy of the sensor output. The default 'packed' request becomes a visually-lossless 8-bit compressed format, unsuitable for pixel maths. (https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/652-raspberry-pi-camera-module-2/documents/RP-008156-DS-7-picamera2-manual.pdf (Raw streams, Pi 5))
- Pi 5 supports temporal denoise (TDN), which accumulates over frames. NoiseReductionMode can be set Off. Picamera2 also exposes a read-only per-frame 'Lux' estimate. For 50 Hz mains the flicker period is 10,000 µs (100 Hz). (https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/652-raspberry-pi-camera-module-2/documents/RP-008156-DS-7-picamera2-manual.pdf)
- The IMX500 PiSP tuning file has black_level 4096 (16-bit scale), rpi.contrast with ce_enable: 1 (adaptive contrast enhancement) and a non-linear gamma curve, TDN parameters in rpi.denoise, and an rpi.lux reference of 950 lux at 10,369 µs and gain 2.0 giving Y = 12,457. (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raspberrypi/libcamera/main/src/ipa/rpi/pisp/data/imx500.json)
- libcamera's Lux estimate = (t_ref/t) x (gain_ref/gain) x (aperture ratio)^2 x (Y/Y_ref) x lux_ref. It works with manual exposure, so the LED-off frame can log an ambient estimate. Its absolute accuracy depends on scene reflectance (UNVERIFIED). (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raspberrypi/libcamera/main/src/ipa/rpi/controller/rpi/lux.cpp)
- In Raspberry Pi tuning files, rpi.contrast does the gamma correction and becomes 'somewhat adaptive' when ce_enable is 1. Processed ISP output is therefore non-linear and scene-dependent. (https://git.fa-fo.de/rahix/openflexure-microscope-server/src/branch/fafo/apidocs/dev/picamera-tuning-files.md ; https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/camera/raspberry-pi-camera-guide.pdf)
- Reference illuminances: office lighting 320-500 lx; overcast day 1,000 lx; full daylight (not direct sun) 10,000-25,000 lx; direct sunlight 32,000-100,000 lx. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lux)
- Incident-light exposure equation N^2/t = E·S/C, with C ≈ 250 for a flat receptor (ISO 2720). (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_meter)
- Inverse-square law and cosine law for illuminance. A Lambertian emitter of radiance L and area A radiates πAL in total, so on-axis intensity I0 = Φ/π (derivation). The point-source approximation holds beyond ~5x the source size. (https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/CVonline/LOCAL_COPIES/RYER/ch06.html)
- Typical 12 V 3-LED 5730 injection module listing: 0.72 W, 60 mA, 110 lm (vendor claim). The team's own doc notes 0.72/0.96/1.2/1.5 W variants, and the owned part is unmeasured. (https://poliledsigns.com/shop/20-modules-3dots-leds-red-module-smd5730-12v-0-72watts-60ma-110lum-ip67-pvc-shell-78x15x5mm ; <repo>/docs/research/2026-11-05_enclosure_power_lights.md)
- (calc) Six modules at 60-110 lm, 70 mm off-axis, 200 mm high (d = 212 mm), toed in: E ≈ 2.4-4.4 klux at the plate centre. The task's hypothetical of 18 LEDs at 50-100 lm each would give ≈6-12 klux. Each 10 mm of distance error changes E by about ±10%. (calc using Ryer cosine/inverse-square relations (https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/CVonline/LOCAL_COPIES/RYER/ch06.html) and the module flux above)
- (calc) LED-dominance ratio r = E_ambient/E_LED, assuming 2.4-4.4 klux from the LEDs: office 0.11-0.21; overcast 1 klux 0.23-0.42; full daylight 10-25 klux 2.3-10; direct sun 32-100 klux 7-42. For specular metal the true r is set by the reflected luminance of lamps or windows, not by lux, so it must be measured from the LED-off frame. (calc from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lux and the LED estimate above)
- (calc) Exposure scale: from the tuning-file Lux reference (950 lx, 10.37 ms, gain 2 gives mean Y ≈ 19% of full scale), 4.4 klux, 10 ms and gain 1 give mean Y ≈ 42% full scale on a reference-like scene. Bright diffuse plates or white developer will therefore likely saturate at 10 ms, and the LEDs will need dimming. The ISO-100 incident-meter estimate (base ISO UNVERIFIED) gives 1.8-3.3 ms for mid-grey at 2.4-4.4 klux. (calc from https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raspberrypi/libcamera/main/src/ipa/rpi/pisp/data/imx500.json and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_meter)
- (calc) Shot-noise SNR of the LED-only signal after on/off subtraction, with the LED at 75% of a 9,610 e- full well: r=0.1 → 74; 0.3 → 59; 1 → 35; 2 → 22; 3 → 16; 7 → 7.8; 10 → 5.6; 25 → 2.3; 40 → 1.5. A 20% defect contrast in direct sun therefore has SNR below ~1.5. (calc using the Sony full-well figure (https://sony.mediaroom.com/2020-05-14-Sony-to-Release-Worlds-First-Intelligent-Vision-Sensors-with-AI-Processing-Functionality))
- Ambient-rejection options in machine vision: high-power short strobes overpower ambient light; physical enclosures are 'the best choice' when strobing is impossible and colour is needed; a narrow bandpass filter is ineffective with white light. (https://machinevisiondirect.com/pages/considerations-for-an-optimal-lighting-solution)
- (calc, physics) With a continuously-on LED, the LED and ambient signals both scale linearly with exposure time, so a shorter exposure does not change their ratio. Shorter exposure helps only when the LED is strobed or overdriven during the exposure, or to avoid saturation. (calc; consistent with the strobe rationale in https://machinevisiondirect.com/pages/considerations-for-an-optimal-lighting-solution)
- Alternate-frame pulsed-LED ambient cancellation, subtracting the LED-off frame from the LED-on frame, was proposed by NASA for active-illumination imaging (summary from the search abstract; full record not fetched). (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110016693)
- A pulsed-illumination occupant-sensing patent states that the pulsed source's signal electrons should exceed the ambient photon noise by about 10x, and that off-chip subtraction needs extra dynamic range to avoid saturation. (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6904347)
- Petschnigg et al. (SIGGRAPH 2004): the difference F − A is meaningful only in linear space (RAW converted to linear, scaled for ISO and exposure). Flash/no-flash pairs are hard to register because illumination differs; they mostly used a tripod. Flash specularities were detected as luminance above 95% of the sensor range. (https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall14/cos526/papers/petschnigg04.pdf)
- India's grid reference frequency is 50.000 Hz with an allowable band of 49.90-50.05 Hz (IEGC 2023, cited in CERC order 2/SM/2025). (calc) Phase drift over an 80 ms burst is ≤0.16 ms, about 1.6% of the 10 ms flicker period. (https://cercind.gov.in/2025/orders/2-SM-2025.pdf)
- DOE flicker fact sheet: percent flicker of conventional sources is at most ~40% (magnetic-ballast CFL 37%; incandescent ~6.6-13.4%). LED products range from 0.4% to ~99-100%, and some show periodic frequencies not seen in conventional sources. (https://www.usailighting.com/stuff/contentmgr/files/1/e18f88d078a54622682528e496596192/misc/flickerfactsheet_doe.pdf)
- (calc) Rolling-shutter switching window = T_frame − (T_readout + E). With E = 10 ms and readout ≤ 33.3 ms (bounded by the 30 fps mode; actual readout UNVERIFIED), the window is −10 ms at 30 fps, −3.3 ms at 25 fps and +6.7 ms at 20 fps. At 25-30 fps every LED switch contaminates part of one frame unless that frame is discarded or flushed. (calc from the mode list at https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html and the picamera2 SensorTimestamp definition)
- Pi 5 GPIO switching latency from Python (lgpio/gpiozero) was not found as a measured figure. One article says lgpio is ~1000x slower than C-level toggling. Any plausible value (µs to sub-ms, UNVERIFIED) is small against a 40 ms frame. (https://i-programmer.info/programming/148-hardware/16985-pi-iot-in-python-using-gpio-zero-pins.html?start=2)
- A test-device patent for image stabilisers models single-hand tremor at about 2°/s, with harmonics from 0.1 Hz up to ~20 Hz. (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8670039)
- (calc) Image shift = f·θ. At 2°/s: 0.53 px binned in a 10 ms exposure (blur), 2.1 px per 40 ms frame, 17 px over a 0.32 s burst. At 0.5°/s (braced): 4.3 px over 0.32 s. DoF at 200 mm, F1.79, CoC 1 binned px (3.1 µm) ≈ 19.8 mm total (±10 mm). Scale 0.131 mm/px binned (0.065 full); FOV 265 x 199 mm. (calc from f = 4.74 mm, F1.79, 1.55 µm (https://www.waveshare.com/Raspberry-Pi-AI-Camera.htm))
- ECC image alignment (Evangelidis & Psarakis, IEEE TPAMI 2008) is invariant to photometric distortions in contrast and brightness, but only global ones. A change of lighting direction (L vs R vs OFF) is not covered, so align frames lit the same way (B1 vs B2). (https://www.academia.edu/5287415/Parametric_Image_Alignment_Using_Enhanced_Correlation_Coefficient_Maximization)
- (calc) For a flat mirror-like plate, a pod 70 mm off-axis at camera height produces its highlight at the half-way point, ~35 mm from centre, i.e. on a 100 mm plate. Left and right pods put their highlights on opposite sides. Tilting the device ~5° or ~10° moves highlights by roughly 17 mm or 35 mm. (calc (pod offset 70 mm from <repo>/docs/research/2026-11-05_enclosure_power_lights.md))
- Feris, Raskar, Tan & Turk (SIBGRAPI 2004): specular spots move with the light position. Taking the per-pixel median of image gradients over multi-flash images suppresses specularities, because a highlight appears in only a minority of the images. (https://merl.com/publications/TR2004-098)
- A photometric-stereo inspection of shiny machined metal parts (four light directions; RGB composite of texture, range and curvature fed to a segmentation CNN) reached Dice 0.83-0.97. The composite beat texture-only input for every coating. The authors attribute the gain to shiny surfaces creating bright regions and deep shadows that hide defects. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8838491/)
- Metallic sheen can be suppressed with a polarised light source plus a polariser on the lens. Metal needs filters on both. The lens filter is rotated until crossed, and 'a lot of light is absorbed'. (https://www.vision-doctor.com/en/filters/polarising-filters.html ; https://paulbourke.net/miscellaneous/crosspolarisation/index.html)
- Edmund Optics India XP38 50x50 mm linear polarising film: Rs 1,791 each (qty 1-10); single 38.5%, parallel 30.1%, crossed <0.004%; 0.19 mm thick; 400-700 nm, so it does not cover the 395 nm UV. A 300x300 mm XP42 sheet costs Rs 20,481. (https://www.edmundoptics.in/p/50-x-50mm-38-transmission-linear-polarizing-film-xp38/47448/ ; https://www.edmundoptics.in/p/300-x-300mm-linear-polarizing-film-xp42-18/52979/)
- (calc) Cross-polarised throughput for a fully depolarised diffuse return ≈ 0.385 x 0.385 ≈ 0.15 (2.75 stops), and lower for metal, whose diffuse lobe is only partly depolarised (UNVERIFIED). Unpolarised ambient still passes the lens film at 0.385, so cross-pol worsens r by ~2.6x. Covering two 77 mm-long pods plus the lens with genuine film needs ~5 pieces, ≈ Rs 9k. A disc on the enclosure window avoids any need to fit the lens barrel (barrel diameter UNVERIFIED). (calc from https://www.edmundoptics.in/p/50-x-50mm-38-transmission-linear-polarizing-film-xp38/47448/)
- MVTec AD 2: every scene was captured under at least four lighting conditions. On the mixed-lighting test split, AU-PRO fell for some methods (MSFlow 24.3% to 11.9%; EfficientAD 30.8% to 19.2%) and barely for others (RD 26.4% to 25.0%), i.e. lighting shift is a major failure mode. (https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21622v1)
- Honzátko et al. (MVA 2021): in multi-illumination defect segmentation, naive rotations can degrade performance by breaking the relation between light angle and image. Illumination-preserving augmentation gave up to +51% AuPRC over standard rotations. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00138-021-01244-z)
- CLAHE (Zuiderveld 1994) limits AHE's noise amplification through a clip limit. Multi-scale Retinex with colour restoration (Jobson et al. 1997) targets dynamic-range compression and colour consistency. No clean ablation isolating CLAHE or Retinex gains for YOLO on NEU-DET/GC10 was found (UNVERIFIED either way). (https://www.cse.unr.edu/~bebis/CS474/StudentPaperPresentations/1994%20-%20CLAHE.pdf ; https://doi.org/10.1109/83.597272)
- Grey-world white balance assumes the scene average is achromatic and overcompensates when one colour dominates (e.g. an orange tint). A large red-dye area is such a case, so use fixed gains calibrated under the device's own LEDs. (https://acorn.stanford.edu/psych221/projects/2010/JasonSu/grayworld.html)
- Ultralytics defaults: hsv_h 0.015, hsv_s 0.7, hsv_v 0.4. With albumentations installed it auto-applies Blur, MedianBlur, ToGray and CLAHE at p=0.01, replaceable via a custom augmentations list. (https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/yolo-data-augmentation/)
- Albumentations includes PlanckianJitter, RandomShadow, RandomSunFlare, RandomGamma, RandomBrightnessContrast, CLAHE, ISONoise, Illumination and RandomToneCurve. The original MIT repo is archived; its successor AlbumentationsX is AGPL-3.0 or commercial. (https://github.com/albumentations-team/albumentations)
- Domain randomisation (Tobin et al. 2017) trains on simulator images with randomised textures and lighting so that real images look like just another variation. Synthetic Blender-generated steel-defect textures have been trained on and tested on real Severstal data. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06907 ; https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/13/7/1176)
- Blender Cycles Light Groups give per-light-group combined passes from one render. So L-only, R-only and world/ambient-only images can be rendered once and recombined. (https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/render/layers/passes.html)
- Ultralytics/YOLOv5 TTA (flip plus 3 scales) raised COCO mAP 0.504 → 0.516 but raised inference time from 22.4 to 80.6 ms (about 3.6x; 2-3x typical). (https://docs.ultralytics.com/yolov5/tutorials/test_time_augmentation/)
- Smartphone lux-meter apps can deviate from reference meters by up to ~60%, and calibration cuts that to ~10%. Another test found up to 113% deviation, with identical phones disagreeing. (https://www.daaam.info/Downloads/Pdfs/science_books_pdfs/2022/Sc_Book_2022-003.pdf ; https://dial.de/en-GB/news/luxmeter-app-vs-measuring-device ; https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/7/6/616)
- Variance-of-Laplacian/gradient focus measures (Pech-Pacheco et al., ICPR 2000) are standard sharpness scores. Thresholds are scene-dependent and must be calibrated per material. (https://bibbase.org/network/publication/pechpacheco-cristbal-chamorromartnez-fernndezvaldivia-diatomautofocusinginbrightfieldmicroscopyacomparativestudy-2000)
- (calc) Wilson 95% CI for recall with about 30 defects per condition is wide: 27/30 gives 0.74-0.97, 54/60 gives 0.80-0.95. Per-condition differences under ~0.1 cannot be resolved with a small test set. (calc (Wilson score interval))

## Recommendations

- CAMERA CONFIG (once, at start): binned 2028x1520 sensor mode; raw stream in an UNPACKED format (SRGGB10, which Pi 5 delivers as bit-exact 16-bit); AeEnable=False, AwbEnable=False; ExposureTime=10000 µs; AnalogueGain fixed (1.0-2.0, set at calibration); ColourGains from a grey card under the LEDs; FrameDurationLimits=(40000,40000); NoiseReductionMode=Off. Check every frame's metadata (ExposureTime, AnalogueGain, FrameDuration ≈ 40000) and discard frames that do not match.
- EXPOSURE RULE: keep exposure at a whole multiple of 10 ms indoors (10/20/30 ms, as in docs/Phase1_build_plan.md) so each row integrates whole 100 Hz flicker cycles. If a bright matte plate saturates at 10 ms, dim the LEDs rather than shortening the exposure. Use hardware PWM at 20 kHz or more (≥200 cycles per exposure, under ~0.5% ripple, calc) or fewer modules. Only go below 10 ms outdoors, where there is no mains flicker. For dark scale, step up to 20 or 30 ms.
- CAPTURE BURST per inspection (≈0.4 s, calc): B1 (both pods) → L (left only) → A (all off) → R (right only) → B2 (both). For each state: switch GPIO, call picamera2 capture_request(flush=True), copy the raw, release. Never reuse a frame that was exposing while the LED state changed.
- PROCESSING in linear raw (float32): Lc = L − A and Rc = R − A (black level cancels); motion check B1 vs B2 with phase correlation or ECC translation on the downsampled green channel; warp by interpolated translation if 1-3 px, RETAKE above 3 px. Self-test: B − A ≈ Lc + Rc. Flat-field each pod with a gain map from (ON − OFF) of a matte white card at 200 mm. Specular-free image M = min(Lc, Rc). Mask pixels at 95% of full scale or more in any frame. Demosaic, apply fixed WB, normalise by the ROI 99th percentile, fixed gamma, 8-bit, then YOLO.
- Keep (Lc − Rc)/(Lc + Rc) as a relief channel for dents and punch marks, for rule-based use or a later model ablation. If it goes into the network, a horizontal flip must swap L and R and negate the channel (Honzátko 2021). Flip is safe on M alone.
- QUALITY GATE (starting thresholds; recalibrate on pilot data so the false-retake rate in the dark room stays under 5%): ambient ratio r = median_ROI(A)/median_ROI(B − A). r ≤ 0.3 → pass. 0.3-1.0 → pass, flagged 'ambient high'. 1.0-2.0 → pass only if motion ≤ 0.5 px (SNR ≈ 22-35, calc). Above 2.0, or more than 2% of ROI saturated in A → RETAKE 'Too bright: shade the part or fit the hood'.
- QUALITY GATE, continued: median_ROI(B − A) below 10% of full scale → step exposure 20/30 ms, then 'part too dark / too far'. Masked fraction of M above 0.5% of ROI → 'Tilt about 10° and retake'. Variance of Laplacian of M below 0.5x the per-material reference → 'Hold still / refocus'. Measured mm/px outside ±5% of 0.131 → 'Move to 200 mm' (DoF ±10 mm, calc). Aligned B2 − B1 residual above ~3x expected shot noise → 'Lighting changed during capture'.
- Build a cheap clip-on BLACK HOOD/STANDOFF: matte-black printed legs plus blackout cloth or black EVA foam, sized to frame a ~140 mm opening at 200 mm, resting on the part. It is the only measure that handles direct sun (r 7-40, calc), and it also removes hand motion, fixes the ±10 mm DoF and fixes the scale. Test it as condition C6b.
- Do NOT adopt cross-polarisation in Phase 1. Genuine Edmund film is Rs 1,791 per 50 mm piece, about Rs 9k for both pods plus the lens. It costs ~2.75 stops on the diffuse signal and worsens r by ~2.6x (calc). If time permits, demo it on one pod with two pieces (≈Rs 3.6k) as Phase 2 evidence. Mount the analyser disc in a rotatable printed ring on the window, not on the lens barrel.
- NORMALISATION: use only deterministic steps (flat-field, percentile scaling, fixed WB and gamma), identical in training and on the device. No grey-world AWB (red dye), no MSR/homomorphic filtering. Run one A/B ablation, raw M vs CLAHE(M) (clip 2.0, 8x8), applied the same way in train and test, and report it.
- TRAINING RECIPE: fine-tune on your own plates captured ONLY through this pipeline. Use NEU-DET/GC10-DET (grey to 3 channels) for pretraining only. Ultralytics: hsv_h 0.01 (protects red-dye hue), hsv_s 0.3-0.5, hsv_v 0.4. Custom Albumentations list (pin an MIT release): RandomGamma 80-125, RandomBrightnessContrast ±0.2, Illumination linear/corner ±10% (residual ambient and distance falloff), RandomShadow p 0.1, GaussNoise/ISONoise (high-r shot noise), MotionBlur ≤ 3 px p 0.1, small PlanckianJitter p 0.1.

## Risks

- The LED illuminance (2.4-4.4 klux) is a calc from vendor lumen claims. If the real modules are dimmer, every ambient ratio gets proportionally worse. Measure on day 1.
- The IMX500 readout time and base sensitivity are UNVERIFIED. The 'no clean inter-frame gap' conclusion uses an upper bound of ≤33.3 ms, and the exposure estimates assume the tuning-file Lux reference scene.
- Hand motion over a ~0.4 s, 5-frame burst can reach ~17 px (binned, at 2°/s, calc). Without a standoff the retake rate may be high, and the translation-only alignment does not model rotation or tilt. Residual ghosting scales with r.
- Specular metal: lamps or windows reflected in polished steel can saturate the A frame regardless of measured lux. Subtraction cannot recover saturated pixels, so they must be masked and re-angled. Where the L and R highlights overlap (curved parts, larger pods), min(L,R) fails.
- Some LED room lights flicker at frequencies other than 100 Hz (DOE). A 10 ms exposure will not cancel them, and subtraction then depends on the ambient staying unchanged across the burst. The B1/B2 consistency check must catch this.
- Using processed ISP output instead of raw silently breaks subtraction: non-linear gamma, ce_enable=1 adaptive contrast, and TDN blending across LED states. Code reviews must enforce raw-only maths.
- Direct sun cannot be handled without a hood (SNR ~1.5-8 at r 7-40, calc). Claiming 'works in any lighting' without that caveat would be inaccurate.
- Phone lux apps can be off by 60-113%. Lux values in the report are order-of-magnitude bins only, unless they are cross-checked with a real meter.
- Small test sets give wide CIs (±~0.1 on recall at n≈30). Per-condition differences smaller than that are not statistically meaningful, so say so.
- Aggressive default hue/saturation augmentation (hsv_s 0.7) may wash out the red-dye colour cue, and horizontal flips break any L/R relief channel unless L and R are swapped.
- Dimming via PWM below ~20 kHz, or PWM that is not hardware-timed, can add frame-to-frame intensity jitter that looks like ambient change.
- Cross-polariser parts at genuine-brand prices (about Rs 9k for full coverage) would consume most of the <Rs 10k budget for limited Phase-1 benefit.
