# Test specimens, the Wesaf dye-penetrant process, and datasets/models for training (Bharat AI-SoC 2026-27, Manufacturing, Phase 1 due 23 Nov 2026)

SUMMARY: No public TDS or SDS exists online for the Wesaf kit (Industrybuying sells it as a 3 x 400 ml kit for Rs 1,175, with no instructions). The names "Cleaner-96 / Penetrant-97 / Developer-98" and the "Type II, Method C" class are therefore UNVERIFIED. Follow the standard solvent-removable visible-dye procedure until the can labels are read: pre-clean, then a 10 min penetrant dwell for cracks in aluminium (NASA Table 3, 16-52 C), keeping the penetrant wet. Remove the excess with a dry cloth, then a cloth damp with cleaner (never flush). Apply a thin developer coat from 20-30 cm and read it at 10-60 min. One batch takes about 35-45 min (calc). Comparable Magnaflux SDSs give the hazards: the developer is an extremely flammable aerosol with a -6 C flash point, made of IPA, acetone and propane/butane; the penetrant carries an azo dye that is a skin sensitiser. So spray outdoors or by an open window, several metres from the Pi, power supplies and LEDs, and use nitrile gloves and safety glasses. Visible red penetrants use azo dyes (Sudan-type, absorbing near 520 nm) that are essentially non-fluorescent; only dedicated "dual-mode" products (Magnaflux SKL-4C, Ardrox 9VF2) also glow orange under UV. A 5-minute UV test with controls will settle it for Penetrant-97. For defects: the auto centre punch gives graded dents, drills give pore-like blind holes, and a scriber, file and sandpaper give graded scratches and abrasion. A hacksaw gives edge slits (crack-like notches, not cracks); salt plus vinegar (chemistry like ASTM G85 A1) gives corrosion; oil, fingerprints and water spots give removable contamination. A real tight crack in 3 mm plate needs either a tight over-bend, which only works on hard tempers such as 6061-T6, 2024 or 7075 and not on 1xxx/5xxx, or reverse-bend fatigue of a notched 20 mm strip; 2024 quench-cracking (the ASME comparator block) is a stretch goal. Do a test bend first to identify the alloy. The AI Camera at its 20 cm minimum focus gives about 64 um/px (calc), so make defects at least about 0.3 mm wide, rely on raking-light glints for fine scratches, and get ground-truth depth from a sectioned punch-calibration coupon rather than from a dial gauge. Public datasets are steel or coated-profile domains with weak transfer to bare aluminium. NEU-DET (research-use) and GC10-DET (licence unclear) suit pretraining and ablations; KSDD/KSDD2 and MVTec AD (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) suit benchmarking the anomaly pipeline; none can go into the repo. The team's own images must carry the training. For the edge, the most defensible Phase-1 design uses anomalib 2.7.0 PatchCore with a ResNet18 backbone, trained only on good plates. It runs on the Pi 5 CPU at about 1.5-2 s per plate, warped to 1024 px (calc, based on Arm's measured 88 ms FP32 ResNet-50 on Pi 5 ExecuTorch). A small YOLO (yolo11n/yolo26n) trained on about 300 auto-labelled images handles the defect type, and the Hailo backbone or YOLO HEF can follow once an x86 Linux host is available for the DFC. Expect about 350 images in one capture day. If labels are drawn once per face in plate millimetre coordinates and projected through a homography, labelling takes hours, not days. With only about 50 physical defects, all accuracy claims must be per-defect, split by plate, with Wilson 95% intervals (for example, 13/15 detected means 62-96%).

- [high] Magnaflux Spotcheck (solvent-removable visible dye) procedure: (1) Pre-clean with cleaner, wipe, let dry, and clean on the same day. (2) Dwell 10-30 min, longer for very tight cracks; re-clean if the penetrant beads up. (3) Wipe with a dry cloth, then with cloths pre-moistened with cleaner; flushing with cleaner impairs sensitivity. (4) Spray a thin, even developer coat from 8-12 in. (5) Large cracks show immediately; small ones take a few minutes. (https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0717/5870/9039/files/MAG02-Magnaflux-Spotcheck_Operating_Instructions_10-14.pdf)
- [high] NASA PT-TE-1426 Table 3: minimum dwell for cracks in aluminium (all forms) is 10 min, at 16-52 C. Penetrant must not dry during dwell. Photograph before post-cleaning. Post-clean because residues attract moisture and cause corrosion. Work in a well-ventilated area. (https://extapps.ksc.nasa.gov/Reliability/Documents/Preferred_Practices/1426.pdf)
- [medium] ASME V Article 6 Table T-672 (secondary copy): aluminium welds, castings, cracks, porosity: 5 min penetrant dwell and 10 min developer time. (https://www.scribd.com/doc/275161683/ASME-v-Article-6)
- [medium] Final interpretation should be made 10-60 min after developer application (RDSO procedure citing ASME V T-676). (https://www.jssl.in/uploads/rdso/dpt.pdf)
- [high] EN/ISO classification has three penetrant types: colour contrast, fluorescent and dual purpose. There are 5 removal methods (water, lipophilic, solvent, hydrophilic, water+solvent) and 5 developer forms. Fluorescent is inherently more sensitive than colour contrast, and non-aqueous developer is the most sensitive form. (https://www.twi-global.com/technical-knowledge/faqs/faq-what-penetrant-systems-are-available-for-carrying-out-dye-penetrant-inspection)
- [high] Representative visible red penetrant SDS (Magnaflux SKL-SP2 aerosol, v5.0, 2024): hazards H223, H229, H304, H317. Its dye is a disazo 2-naphthalenol (azo) dye at 1-3%. Gloves: nitrile 0.38 mm, more than 480 min breakthrough. Eyes: safety glasses EN166. (https://assets.testequity.com/te1/Documents/pdf/magnaflux/Magnaflux_SKL-SP2-Aerosol_Safety-Data-Sheet_0826.pdf)
- [high] Representative non-aqueous developer SDS (Magnaflux SKD-S2): hazards H222/H229 (extremely flammable aerosol), H319 and H336. Contents: propan-2-ol 25-40%, butane, propane, isobutane, acetone. Flash point -6 C. Keep below 50 C (P410+P412); store at 10-30 C. (https://rapidwelding.com/files/SKD-S2-Safety-Data-Sheet.pdf)
- [high] Visible penetrants are non-fluorescent by design. Dual-mode products exist: Magnaflux SKL-4C shows purple-red in visible light and fluorescent orange under UV, and Ardrox 9VF2 is classed as both Type II and Type III. Each mode is weaker than in a dedicated visible or fluorescent product. (https://magnaflux.com/Products/SKL-4C.htm ; https://silmid.com/fr/non-destructive-testing-ndt/penetrants/Ardrox-9VF2-Variant ; https://chemical-biological.tpub.com/TM-1-1500-335-23/css/TM-1-1500-335-23_111.htm)
- [medium] Sudan IV (a typical oil-soluble red azo dye) absorbs maximally at 520 nm and quenches other fluorophores. Inference: a Sudan-type Penetrant-97 is unlikely to fluoresce under 395 nm, and it will look dark under blue or green light against white developer. (https://polysciences.com/products/sudan-iv-25 ; https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/604b5e1a6f90b7f6cac7f14e)

## Recommendations
- Penetrant documentation: photograph all three Wesaf can labels (front and back) for the report, and ask the seller for the TDS/SDS. Until those arrive, use the generic Method C procedure and cite the Magnaflux instructions, NASA PT-TE-1426 and ASME V T-672 as the basis.
- Penetrant procedure for each batch:
- Wipe the plate with Cleaner-96 and let it flash off for 5 min.
- Spray Penetrant-97 and dwell 10 min (allowed range 5-30 min). Keep it wet: re-spray if it starts drying at Bengaluru temperatures.
- Wipe with a dry lint-free cloth, then with a cloth dampened (not dripping) with Cleaner-96. Never spray the cleaner straight onto the plate.
- Dry for 2-5 min.
- Shake the developer until the ball rattles. Spray one thin coat from 20-30 cm in short passes, only until the plate is just white.
- Photograph at 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 min (this doubles as a bleed-out time-lapse). Make the final read between 10 and 60 min.
- Post-clean with cleaner and a cloth. Log temperature, dwell and development times in the inspection record.
- Penetrant safety: spray outdoors or at an open window, at least about 3 m from the Pi, the Hailo HAT, PSUs and LED drivers, with the power off nearby. Let the plates flash off for 5 min or more before they go back under the camera. Cover the camera lens and HAT fan against developer overspray. Wear 5-mil nitrile gloves and change them when wet (they are thinner than the 0.38 mm the SDS rates), plus safety glasses. Store the cans below 30 C, never in a car or in sun. Never mix bleach with vinegar during the corrosion step.
- 5-minute fluorescence bench test (dark room):
- Drive one 395 nm LED from 5 V through 100 ohm, about 17 mA (calc).
- On a bare aluminium offcut and a matte black card, put five spots: penetrant only; penetrant under developer; developer only; a yellow highlighter streak (positive control); clean metal (negative control). Do not use printer paper: optical brighteners fluoresce blue.
- Shoot each spot with the AI Camera at fixed exposure, gain and white balance, UV on and UV off. Optionally hold an orange or yellow plastic sheet over the lens to block the violet LED light.
- Compare the mean R channel in each ROI, UV on minus UV off. If the penetrant's increase is far above clean metal and developer-only (for example, more than 3x; my threshold), Penetrant-97 is usable in dual mode. Otherwise treat it as visible-only.
- Also shoot the developed slit under the blue LED modules. The red dye absorbs near 520 nm, so indications should look dark on the white developer; measure that contrast against white light.
- Order of operations:
- Day 1: make all mechanical defects, then start the corrosion spots at once (24/48/72 h). Mask the rest of the face with tape.
- Measure ground truth.
- Shoot the whole surface-mode dataset.
- Run the removable-contamination sessions on the good plates, then clean them.
- Only then run the penetrant, and only on crack/slit specimens. Trapped red dye would contaminate later surface images. Never put penetrant on the good reference plates.
- Identify the alloy first: cut a 100x20 mm strip and fold its end flat (about 0-1T) in the vise. If it cracks on the outside, the plate is likely 6xxx-T6 or harder and over-bend cracks are possible. If it folds cleanly, it is 1xxx/3xxx/5xxx; then use the fatigue method below.
- Specimen matrix (split by plate, so no physical defect appears in both train and test):
- P1 (faces A/B): GOOD, train. Feeds the PatchCore memory bank and the YOLO background images. Removable contamination on P1-B: fingerprint, machine-oil drop, marker dot, tap-water spot, dust. Clean with Cleaner-96 afterwards.
- P2 (A/B): GOOD, test negatives; contamination test session on P2-B.
- P3 (train): face A carries a dent grid of 3 punch settings x 1/2/3 hits (9 dents), 3 heavy hammer-and-punch dents, and 3 blind holes (1, 2, 3 mm drills, about 0.5-1 mm deep). Face B carries 6 scriber scratches (light/medium/heavy x 1 and 5 passes, 30-60 mm long), 1 needle-file scratch, and 80-grit and 220-grit abrasion patches of 10x10 mm.
- P4 (train): first cut two 100x20 strips, S1 and S2, leaving 100x60. Keep one sawn edge burred and deburr the other. Face A/edges: 3 hacksaw edge slits (3, 6, 10 mm deep), 2 file V-notches, 1 edge chip. Face B: 3 corrosion spots plus 1 stain.
- P5 (test): a smaller copy of every type: 4 dents, 1 hole, 3 scratches, 1 abrasion patch, 2 slits, 1 notch, a burr edge, 1 corrosion spot.
- S1 (real crack): file a 1 mm V-notch at mid-length, clamp it just above the vise jaws, and reverse-bend it about +-15-20 deg by hand. Check every few cycles. Stop when a hairline runs 2-5 mm from the notch root, then unload so the crack closes tight; this is the penetrant demo. Describe it honestly as a 'notch-initiated fatigue crack'.
- S2 (punch calibration): make the same 9 punch marks, saw across them, sand the cut face flat, and take a macro photo beside a ruler to get true depth for each setting.
- Stretch goal: ASME-style quench cracking (heat to about 510-524 C, checked with a crayon, then water-quench). It only works reliably on 2024; there is a melting risk.
- Ground-truth table, one row per defect: ID, plate/face, centre x,y in mm from a marked datum corner, type, tool setting, length, width, depth, measurement method, uncertainty.
- Length: steel rule or caliper.
- Width: phone macro photo with a 0.5 mm-graduated rule in frame; for slits, a feeler gauge.
- Depth: from the S2 section for dents; drill depth for holes.
- Scratch depth: categorical only (light/medium/heavy). A dial gauge's 2-3 mm ball tip is too big for sub-mm dents. Say this in the report.
- Capture protocol: fixed jig at 20 cm with the AI Camera at full 4056x3040. Shoot every face in 4 rotations x 3 placements x 2 light states (room light, and LEDs on with dark-frame subtraction), about 350 images in all. Detect the plate corners in OpenCV and warp each image to a 1024x1024 canonical view at about 98 um/px. Put an orientation mark on one plate edge.
- Labelling: draw each face's defects once in plate-mm coordinates (the GT CSV), project the boxes into every image through the homography, and write YOLO txt files. Import them into Label Studio as pre-annotations for a quick review (pip install label-studio==1.23.2; label-studio start). Expected effort: about 3 h measuring, 3-4 h scripting, 2 h review. Labelling by hand instead takes about 12 person-hours for 350 images (calc). Self-host CVAT, or use Roboflow only if the data may be public.
- Anomaly detector, Phase 1 (CPU, no x86 host needed):
- anomalib 2.7.0 PatchCore: Patchcore(backbone='resnet18', layers=('layer2','layer3'), coreset_sampling_ratio=0.01-0.02, num_neighbors=9).
- Train on 256x256 tiles from P1 (all rotations, placements and lighting) with the Folder datamodule. Test on P2 good tiles plus P5 defect tiles.
- Export the backbone only (ONNX via dynamo, or ExecuTorch XNNPACK, which also strengthens the ExecuTorch story). Keep the memory bank as a .npy file and run kNN per tile in NumPy/FAISS on the Pi, which avoids whole-bank export and memory blow-up.
- Report image- and tile-level AUROC and the measured per-plate latency.

## Risks
- The Wesaf kit's real dwell times, chemistry and hazards are unknown (no TDS/SDS online). => Read the can labels and ask the seller for the TDS/SDS. Meanwhile use the conservative generic procedure (10 min dwell, 10-60 min read). Do a trial run on an offcut slit before the demo plates.
- Fire or ignition: the developer is an extremely flammable aerosol (flash point -6 C) and the bench has a Pi, PSUs and LED drivers. => Spray outdoors or by an open window at least 3 m from the electronics, with no power switching nearby. Let parts flash off for 5 min or more before bringing them back to the rig. Store the cans below 30 C.
- Penetrant residue trapped in pits, scratches and slits contaminates later surface-mode images, or ruins the good reference plates. => Fix the order: surface dataset first, penetrant last, and only on S1/P4/P5. Never put penetrant on P1 or P2. Flag post-penetrant images in the metadata.
- No real tight crack is achievable if the plates are soft 1xxx/5xxx alloy. => Do the bend test on day 1. Use the notched-strip reverse-bend fatigue crack, and label slits as 'crack-like notch'. If 6xxx-T6, add the over-bend crack. The 2024 quench crack is optional only.
- Defects too fine for 64 um/px (hairline scratches under 1 px) make the detector look poor. => Size the specimen defects at 0.3 mm or more. Use raking LED light so scratches glint. Check whether the lens focuses below 20 cm. State the resolution limit in the report.
- Data leakage: rotations and placements of the same defect land in both train and test, inflating accuracy. => Split by plate (P1/P3/P4 train; P2/P5 test). Count unique physical defects, not images, and report Wilson CIs.
- The test set is too small for confident numbers (about 15 defects gives CIs of roughly +-17%). => Put every defect type on the test plate P5 and, if time allows, on the S strips. Use public-benchmark AUROC as the second line of evidence. Phrase results as prototype results.
- The Hailo DFC needs x86_64 Linux; the team has only a Mac, so the HEF compile could block the schedule. => Treat CPU inference (PatchCore via ExecuTorch/ONNX, YOLO via ONNX/NCNN) as the Phase-1 baseline. Use a rented x86 VM or Ultralytics Platform managed export for the YOLO HEF. Set a go/no-go on about 18 Nov.
- anomalib 2.7.0's mandatory dynamo exporter, or exporting the whole memory bank, fails or uses too much memory on the Pi. => Export only the backbone and do kNN in NumPy/FAISS, tile by tile. Shrink the coreset to 1-2% or project features to 128-d.
- Licences: CC BY-NC-SA datasets and AGPL-3.0 Ultralytics carry obligations for an open-source submission and for any later product. => Keep datasets out of the repo and use download scripts. License the repo AGPL-3.0 if YOLO is used, or pick a permissive detector. Base Phase-2 product models on own data only.
- CPU contention: PatchCore kNN and Llama (ExecuTorch) both want all four A76 cores, adding latency spikes. => Run them in sequence: vision finishes, then Llama generates. Pin threads, and report a per-stage latency breakdown.
- Corrosion on aluminium may be too slow or too faint with salt+vinegar in 3 days. => Start on day 1 under wet tissue in a sealed box, with 5% NaCl in vinegar (about pH 3, as in ASTM G85 A1). Keep it moist, check daily, and accept white pitted spots as 'corrosion'.
- The UV fluorescence test gives false positives from violet LED reflection or a fluorescent developer. => Use the R channel with UV on vs off, a clean-metal control and a developer-only control, and an orange/yellow filter. Avoid paper with optical brighteners. Wear UV-blocking glasses and never look into 395 nm LEDs.
