# Public steel/metal surface-defect datasets (bbox), licences, nano-YOLO baselines, transfer evidence, and a 4-6 class set the team can make and photograph on mild-steel / stainless / aluminium plates at about 0.064 mm/px under varying lighting

(Research, 14 Nov 2026. Every finding carries its source; UNVERIFIED and (calc) are marked.)

## Summary

DECISION: use 5 detector classes: scratch, dent, pit, corrosion, stain (stain covers oil, dried water and fingerprints). Leave crack out of the Phase-1 detector. In public data it is the weakest class: NEU crazing scores 47-56 AP50 with nano YOLOs. A real tight crack is also narrower than a pixel at 0.064 mm/px. Add hacksaw slits or penetrant indications as an optional 6th class only if the physical samples already exist.
PUBLIC DATA: no public set matches bare plates photographed handheld. NEU-DET, GC10-DET, Severstal and X-SDD are all grayscale images from steel strip lines (line-scan or production-line cameras) with mill-scale texture. Use them only for pretraining and an ablation, with this class mapping: NEU scratches -> scratch; NEU pitted_surface + GC10 rolled_pit -> pit; GC10 oil_spot + water_spot -> stain. Drop all other classes, and drop any image that contains them. No steel-strip set has dents or rust. The closest optical match is the painted sheet-metal set (dent and scratch, 0.05 mm/px, CC BY-SA 4.0). CC BY 4.0 Roboflow sets add dent, pit, rust and smudge, but their image counts are UNVERIFIED. The team's own captures have to carry both training and testing.
LICENCES: neither NEU-DET nor GC10-DET states a licence; each only asks for a citation. Roboflow re-uploads tagged CC BY 4.0, MIT or Public Domain cannot grant more rights than the original. KSDD, KSDD2, MVTec AD and MVTec AD 2 are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so use them for evaluation only. Severstal's Kaggle terms could not be read (UNVERIFIED), so skip it; it is segmentation-only and its 4 classes have no names. The Fraunhofer aluminium-plate set (dent, scratch, water stain, fingerprint) is the closest material match, but it has no licence: email the authors before using it. Never commit third-party images to the repo; ship download scripts and a DATA_LICENSES.md instead.
BASELINES TO QUOTE: on NEU-DET, YOLOv5n reaches 76.0 mAP50 (scratches 91.4, pitted 82.7, crazing 40.1). YOLOv8n reaches 73.6 (scratches 89.1, pitted 81.8, crazing 47.6; from a preprint). YOLO11n reaches 78.3 ± 1.1 over 5 seeds. On GC10-DET, YOLOv8n reaches 73.25 (water_spot 87.7, oil_spot 47.7, crease 32.6). NEU-DET has no official split, so numbers differ between papers. Publish your own split and seed.
TRANSFER: no controlled numbers were found for moving from lab steel datasets to handheld photos; this is a gap the team should measure itself. The evidence that does exist points one way. ImageNet-to-steel has a 'significant domain gap', and in-domain pretraining adds up to 10 mAP50. Synthetic-only training scores below real-only (F1px 36.8 vs 47.0), but synthetic pretraining plus a real fine-tune beats real-only (50.3). In MVTec AD 2, lighting shifts keep state-of-the-art methods below 60% AU-PRO.
LIGHTING ('works irrespective of lighting'): build this into the data and the report. Lock exposure, gain and white balance. Capture every defect under a matrix: LED state (both pods / left / right / off) x ambient (dark / room light / daylight / side lamp). Train on the LED-on minus LED-off difference frames. Report precision and recall for each lighting condition, and claim robustness only within the lighting conditions actually tested.
RESOLUTION (calc): YOLO at 640 px on the full 4056 px frame gives 0.40 mm/px, so a 0.3 mm scratch is under 1 px and disappears. Crop to the plate and tile at 0.064-0.08 mm/px: at 0.08 mm/px a 100 mm plate is 1250 px, i.e. 2 x 2 tiles of 640.
HONEST REPORTING: the primary test set is physical plates held out from training. Split by plate, never by image. Report per-defect P/R/F1 with Wilson 95% confidence intervals at a fixed threshold, plus false positives per clean plate, broken down by lighting. Secondary results: (a) a seeded NEU-DET test split, for comparability only and labelled as a different domain; (b) a public-data-only model run on your own test set, to show the domain gap. Aim for at least 20-30 test defects per class: 27/30 detected gives a 74-97% interval (calc).

- ASTM G48 Method A is the ferric chloride pitting test for stainless steels and related alloys. It is an accelerated test, so damage exceeds natural exposure over the same time. A dilute FeCl3 dab is therefore a recognised way to make pits on stainless; the exact concentration and time for 3 mm plate are UNVERIFIED. (https://store.astm.org/g0048-25.html)
- Machine-vision practice: overdriving or strobing the light and shortening the exposure cuts the ambient contribution. In a vendor example, 2x intensity at half the exposure gives about 4x less ambient (vendor claim). (https://www.1stvision.com/machine-vision-solutions/tag/gardasoft)

The LED-on minus LED-off difference image rests on light adding linearly. ## Recommendations

- FINAL CLASS SET (5): scratch / dent / pit / corrosion / stain. (1) SCRATCH: a line at least 4x longer than wide and at least 0.3 mm wide. Make with a scriber, needle file and 80/220-grit paper on all three metals. Public support: NEU scratches (300 images; 85-93 AP50 with nano YOLOs), painted sheet-metal scratch (CC BY-SA), Roboflow dank1ng scratch. (2) DENT: a rounded depression about 1.5 mm or wider. Make with the auto centre punch at 3 settings x 1-3 hits, plus hammer-and-punch for big dents. Public support: painted sheet-metal dent/elevation, dank1ng dent. A dent is a shape defect, so it needs the separate left-pod and right-pod frames. (3) PIT: a sharp cavity under about 1.5 mm, alone or in clusters. Make with 0.5-1 mm deep blind holes from a 1-2 mm drill, a light single punch, dilute FeCl3 on stainless (ASTM G48-type chemistry) or salt plus vinegar on aluminium (white pits). Public support: NEU pitted_surface (about 82 AP50), GC10 rolled_pit (only 85 boxes), dank1ng pit. (4) CORROSION: red-brown rust on mild steel, white corrosion product on aluminium. Make with a salt and white-vinegar tissue for 24-72 h, starting today (Sat 14 Nov). Public support is weak: CC BY 4.0 Roboflow steel-corrosion sets (counts UNVERIFIED), dank1ng rust-spot. (5) STAIN (removable contamination): a drop of machine oil, a dried tap-water ring, a fingerprint. Llama's advice for it is 'clean and re-inspect' rather than reject. Public support: GC10 oil_spot (569 boxes) and water_spot (354 boxes), dank1ng smudge. Expect oil stains to be the hard case: 47.7 AP50 on GC10.
- DROP CRACK from the Phase-1 detector: NEU crazing is the worst class in every nano-YOLO baseline (40-56 AP50), and a real tight crack is narrower than a pixel at 0.064 mm/px. If the team already has hacksaw slits or penetrant indications, add them as an optional 6th class 'slit' trained only on own data, and report it separately. If dent and pit are confused in more than about 20% of cases on validation, merge them into one 'dent' class and let the rule engine split them by diameter in mm.
- SEVERITY stays rule-based and in mm, never confidence. Convert pixels to mm with the known 0.064 mm/px (or the 0.08 mm/px warped grid). Inputs: length or diameter, count per plate, and zone (edge band vs centre). Stain is never a reject on its own; corrosion and pit severity rise with area or count.
- PUBLIC PRETRAINING (stage 1, optional ablation): start from COCO-pretrained YOLO. Pretrain on a mapped subset: NEU-DET scratches and pitted_surface images only; GC10-DET oil_spot, water_spot and rolled_pit images only, dropping any image that also has unmapped classes; painted sheet-metal scratch and dent; dank1ng after a manual quality check. Then fine-tune on own data. Report COCO-only vs public-pretrain-then-own on the same held-out plates. If public pretraining does not help, say so; that is a legitimate finding given the documented domain gap.
- OWN CAPTURES (stage 2, carries the result): use at least 3 metals x at least 2 training plates, plus held-out test plates whose defects are made with the same tools but never seen in training. Image each defect face under a lighting matrix: LED state {both pods, left only, right only, off/ambient} x ambient {dark room, ceiling light, window daylight, desk lamp from one side}, at 2-3 handheld poses (tilt within about 10 deg, 180-220 mm). Lock AeEnable/AwbEnable with fixed ExposureTime, AnalogueGain and ColourGains for each LED state, and keep highlights on stainless and aluminium below saturation. Store both the raw frame and the LED-on minus LED-off difference frame. Train mainly on the difference frames, so that ambient light largely cancels out (assumes linear frames, same exposure, no clipping).
- SYNTHETIC (stage 3, only if time allows): copy-paste own defect crops onto clean-plate images, with photometric augmentation (brightness, gamma, colour temperature, synthetic specular blobs, simulated room-light gradients). Use any Blender PBR renders only as pretraining followed by a real fine-tune. The Fraunhofer results show synthetic-only data underperforms, while synthetic pretraining plus real fine-tuning beats real-only.
- RESOLUTION: never feed the whole 4056 px frame to YOLO at 640. Detect the plate, warp it to a 0.064-0.08 mm/px grid, run 640 px tiles with overlap (2 x 2 at 0.08 mm/px), then apply NMS and merge boxes across tile seams. Make physical defects at least 0.3 mm wide.
- LICENSING FOR A PUBLIC GITHUB REPO: commit no third-party images or labels. Add scripts that download from the official sources (NEU faculty page, GC10 GitHub/Baidu, Fairdata DOI), and a DATA_LICENSES.md that records each source's actual terms. Treat Roboflow CC tags on NEU/GC10 copies as unreliable. Use KSDD, KSDD2 and MVTec AD/AD 2 for evaluation only (NC-SA) and keep them out of any product model. Release your own images under CC BY 4.0, or CC BY-SA 4.0 if you redistribute anything mixed with the painted sheet-metal set. Email the Fraunhofer ITWM authors for permission to use the aluminium-plates set, which is the closest domain match. (Ultralytics' AGPL-3.0 obligations were noted in the team's 5 Nov notes; not re-checked here.)
- HONEST DETECTOR REPORTING: (a) PRIMARY: held-out physical plates, split by plate (GroupShuffleSplit with plate ID), never by image or frame. Give per-defect precision, recall and F1 at one fixed confidence threshold chosen on validation, with Wilson 95% CIs, plus false positives per clean plate. Show a per-lighting-condition table and state the worst-case condition. Count defects, not images: 12 lighting frames of one dent are 1 defect. Target at least 20 test defects per class, ideally 30 (27/30 gives 74-97%). (b) SECONDARY: tile-level mAP50 and mAP50:95 on the same plates. (c) COMPARABILITY: NEU-DET on a published seeded 8:1:1 split, labelled 'different domain', quoted next to the YOLOv5n 76.0 / YOLOv8n 73.6 / YOLO11n 78.3 baselines. (d) DOMAIN-GAP DISCLOSURE: the public-data-only model evaluated on your own test plates.
- Label quickly by drawing each face's defects once in plate-mm coordinates and projecting the boxes into every frame through the plate homography, as the 5 Nov notes propose. The same ground-truth CSV then also gives the mm sizes used for severity scoring.

## Risks

- Licence ambiguity: NEU-DET and GC10-DET have no licence, which by default means all rights reserved (legal reading UNVERIFIED). Training on them for a public non-commercial student project is the norm in papers, but redistributing them or using them in a later product is not covered. Mitigate by keeping them out of the repo, citing them, and keeping the product model trainable on own data only.
- Severstal's Kaggle terms could not be read (UNVERIFIED), and the X-SDD download is behind an IEEE DataPort subscription. Do not plan around either.
- Domain gap: public steel sets are grayscale strip-mill or line-scan images with mill-scale texture, while the team's images are RGB or difference frames of bare, partly specular plates. Public pretraining may give little gain or even hurt, and no controlled transfer numbers exist to predict it. The own-data ablation is the only evidence that will count.
- Lighting claims: MVTec AD 2 shows lighting shifts still defeat state-of-the-art methods (below 60% AU-PRO). 'Works irrespective of lighting' must be stated as 'validated across N tested ambient conditions with the enclosure LEDs dominant'. Direct sunlight, or saturation on polished stainless or aluminium, can still break the LED-on minus LED-off difference.
- Class confusions likely at 0.064 mm/px: dent vs pit (size continuum), stain vs shadow or reflection (colour and reflectance under changing ambient light), and corrosion colour shifts if white balance is not locked. Oil stain is weak even in-domain (47.7 AP50 on GC10).
- Small test sets give wide intervals (9/10 detected means 60-98%). Splitting by image instead of by plate leaks near-duplicate frames and inflates P/R.
- Baseline numbers come partly from a preprint (arXiv 2606.07659) and Hugging Face model cards that are not peer-reviewed. NEU-DET has no official split, so values move several points between papers. Present them as context ranges, not as targets.
- Schedule: corrosion needs 24-72 h and FeCl3 pitting needs hours, so start both today. The 12-condition lighting matrix multiplies capture time; budget about one capture day. FeCl3 stains and corrodes, so wear gloves and eye protection and keep it away from the Pi and LEDs.
