# Synthetic data and digital twins for visual surface-defect detection (sim-to-real) on metal and aerospace parts: what the evidence shows, how NVIDIA does it, and what a 2-3 person team can do in about 2 weeks (Pi 5 + Hailo-10H + IMX500, Phase 1 due 23 Nov 2026)

(Research, 5 Nov 2026. Every finding carries its source; UNVERIFIED and (calc) are marked.)

## Summary

The 2020-2026 evidence does not support the idea of training only in simulation and then working directly in the real world for surface defects on metal. Models trained only on synthetic images consistently score below real-only models on real test images. On metal and scratch tasks the measured gap is about 0.15 mAP50 (ScratchSim, toy car), 0.26 AP (SynSur pitting) and 0.45 AP (SynSur scratches). On an aluminium part with real dents and scratches, pixel F1 was about 18% for synthetic-only against 26-31% for real-only (Fulir et al., CVPRW 2023). The approach that works is synthetic pretraining followed by fine-tuning on a small real set. Fulir's aluminium part went from 26-31% to 36-40% F1. ScratchSim went from 0.610 to 0.680-0.723 mAP50, and it avoided the collapse to about 0.05 mAP50 seen when only 10-25% of the real data was used. Once enough real data exists, the gains shrink to nothing (SynSur MSD). Fine scratches, corrosion and specular glare on shiny metal are where synthetic data does worst. This shows up in BladeSynth, in ScratchSim, and in NVIDIA's own 2023 Replicator scratch example, which reported initial scores of 74%, 34% and 39% (metric not stated), missed long scratches, and flagged reflective edges as false positives. Dents and nicks transfer best. The aerospace record is thin. BladeSynth checked sim-to-real only qualitatively, on 10 real blades. The zeroG/Lufthansa A320 hangar simulation reported no results on real images. DLR's fuselage dent work mixed real and synthetic images (YOLOv11 mAP50 0.66) without separating out what the synthetic part contributed. Boeing holds patents on synthetic anomaly images, and GE and Rolls-Royce disclose no synthetic-trained inspection models. What closes the gap: copy the real acquisition geometry and lights exactly; use procedural surface textures tuned to the real part with randomised roughness, normal strength and scale; place objects with physics; randomise textures for reflective parts; add camera-effect and noise models; label defects by whether they are visible, not just whether they exist (otherwise over-labelling hurts); and fine-tune on real images. One to five real images recovered most of the gap in an object-detection benchmark, but defects are harder than objects. NVIDIA's own stack is not usable on the team's Mac: Isaac Sim/Replicator needs x86 Ubuntu or Windows 11 with an RTX GPU that has RT cores (current minimum RTX 4080 16 GB; A100/H100 not supported). The Omniverse Launcher was deprecated on 1 Oct 2025, and NVIDIA's defect-generation extension was archived on 8 Jul 2026. Cosmos AnomalyGen was tested only on A100/H100/RTX 6000 under Linux. For 2 weeks the realistic plan is a "digital twin of the inspection cell" in Blender (Cycles runs on Apple Metal): the 100x100x3 mm plate or aircraft-skin coupon, the IMX500 optics at about 65 um/px, and the real LED pod geometry. Render 2,000-3,000 tiles at 640x640 with domain randomisation, use them only for pretraining and an ablation, and let real captures from the five plates carry training, calibration and all the accuracy claims, tested on a plate the model never saw. The Llama QC assistant cannot be "trained in simulation" in NVIDIA's sense. The twin can, however, generate structured defect records to test the advice prompts, so the assistant gets a simulation test that way.

## Findings

- Fulir et al. (CVPRW 2023), aluminium clutch: dents (0.2-2 mm) and scratches (0.05-0.3 mm) were modelled into the 3D geometry, with procedural surface textures tuned to the real part. Viewpoints and ring light matched the real robot setup, and uniform noise was added to imitate sensor dark noise. Data: SynthClutch, 40 instances x 106 viewpoints = 4,240 images; RealClutch, 6 objects, 516 images. Pixel F1 on the real test set (FCN/DeepLabV3/U-Net): real-only 28.4/25.8/31.3; synthetic-only 18.1/18.1/18.7 (recall only about 10.7%); synthetic pretraining + real fine-tune 35.7/36.3/40.5. (https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/VISION/papers/Fulir_Synthetic_Data_for_Defect_Segmentation_on_Complex_Metal_Surfaces_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf)
- Fulir et al.: public planar defect datasets (DAGM, KSDD2, Severstal, MTD, CSEM-MISD) gave near-zero transfer to the aluminium part without fine-tuning (F1 0.1-13.9%). ImageNet pretraining did not help. Synthetic data was 'over-labelled': defects that were geometrically visible but not visually prominent were labelled, which lowers recall. Tiny bright irregularities on the real part caused false positives that fine-tuning did not fully fix. (https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/VISION/papers/Fulir_Synthetic_Data_for_Defect_Segmentation_on_Complex_Metal_Surfaces_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf)
- ScratchSim (arXiv 2607.27065, 2026): BlenderProc/Cycles with PBR, scratches drawn as Bezier curves, a viewing-angle visibility threshold, and 10,000 synthetic 640x640 images. Real set: 116 webcam images of a toy car (test set only 16). YOLO26 mAP50: real-only 0.610; synthetic-only 0.451; mixed with 10% real 0.507; synthetic then real fine-tune 0.680; fine-tune from white-background synthetic 0.723. Real-only with 10% or 25% of the real data collapsed to 0.056 and 0.043. Fine, low-contrast scratches stayed hard under every strategy. (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27065)
- ScratchSim, industrial grip (YOLOX-S): real AP 0.234; best synthetic-only 0.196; best fine-tuned 0.319. The worst synthetic configuration scored only 0.011, so the rendering and randomisation choices decide whether synthetic data helps at all. (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27065)
- SynSur (arXiv 2604.26633, 2026), diffusion (Flux+LoRA) inpainting, not rendering. BSData pitting, YOLOv26 AP: real-only 0.652, synthetic-only 0.393, union 0.667 (+0.015). MSD scratches: real-only 0.953, synthetic-only 0.500, no gain from adding synthetic. Conclusion: synthetic-only does not replace real data. (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26633)
- SynthRender/I-AsSET (arXiv 2602.21141, 2026; BlenderProc + Cycles; this is object detection, not defects, so treat it as an upper bound). With 4,000 synthetic images, synthetic-only reached 95.1 mAP50 / 82.9 mAP50:95 on 508 real images. Adding 1/3/5/10 real images gave 97.1/97.9/98.5/98.8 mAP50. Doubling to 8,000 images added only +0.3. In the ablation, physics-based placement mattered most, and randomised textures are recommended for reflective parts. (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21141)
- BladeSynth (Scientific Data, 19 Jul 2025): aero-engine blades rendered with PBR from a 3D-scanned mesh, randomised lighting, camera and textures, with procedural corrosion, nicks, dents and scratches. Even on synthetic test data, scratches and corrosion had lower recall and mAP than dents and nicks. F1 rose from 0.79 at 5% of the data to 0.84 at 50%, then plateaued. Sim-to-real was checked only qualitatively, on 10 real defective blades. (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-05563-y)
- Schaller, Li and Jenkins (JETA, Feb 2025), aero-engine borescope: models trained only on collage/DCGAN synthetic images performed poorly on real images. The authors recommend training on both synthetic and real images. (https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-36ab9d95f8bb4292a75b04ed4efc07fc/Description)
- DLR (Mhatre et al., CEAS-AIDAA 2025, Materials Research Proceedings vol. 69, 2026): aircraft fuselage dent detection on a mixed real + synthetic dataset gave YOLOv11 mAP50 0.66 and RT-DETR 0.57. The paper does not separate out the synthetic contribution, and the record lists it as not peer-reviewed. (https://elib.dlr.de/221397)
- DLR/Uni Hamburg thesis (vom Schemm): dents usually have no distinguishing colour and show only through shadows or specular reflections. A specular-reflection capture setup on real aircraft surfaces gave excellent dent detection with RT-DETR and YOLOv12. The lighting geometry matters more than the model. (https://elib.dlr.de/220402/1/Master-Thesis-Ronja-vom-Schemm.pdf)
- zeroG (Lufthansa group): an A320 hangar scene in UE4 + AirSim, with virtual scratches, dents and lightning-strike burns and auto-generated masks for Mask R-CNN. They claim >95% detection only inside the simulation. Transfer to real drone footage was not demonstrated. (https://www.zerog.aero/case-study/drone-aircraft-exterior-inspection)
- Boeing patent US 12,039,009 (filed 5 Aug 2021, granted 16 Jul 2024) covers rendering synthetic images of aircraft surface abnormalities under predefined lighting and viewpoints to train ML. A separate Boeing patent, US 11,599,986 (granted 7 Mar 2023), uses GAN translation between optical and thermal images. (https://patents.justia.com/patent/12039009 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US11599986)
- GE Aerospace rolled out an AI Blade Inspection Tool for LEAP MRO in 2025. Rolls-Royce's 'Intelligent Borescope' (with Waygate) relies on templated real imaging and historical data. Neither publicly discloses synthetic-trained models. (https://www.geaerospace.com/news/press-releases/ge-aerospace-deploys-ai-driven-inspection-tool-maximize-narrowbody-engine-time-wing ; https://www.rolls-royce.com/media/our-stories/discover/2025/intelligent-borescope-method.aspx)
- NVIDIA technical blog (19 Apr 2023): Omniverse Replicator projects scratch normal maps (made in Substance Designer) onto a CAD part via a proxy cube, with random location, size and rotation. YOLOv8 was trained on 1,000 synthetic images. Initial scores were 74%, 34% and 39% (metric not stated). Long scratches were missed, and reflective edges and floor scratches were detected. Manually annotated real images were then added. (https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-to-train-a-defect-detection-model-using-synthetic-data-with-nvidia-omniverse-replicator)
- NVIDIA's kit-extension-sample-defectsgen (Apache-2.0, tested with Omniverse Code 2022.3.3+) projects a single image-based defect onto a prim. The repository was archived (read-only) on 8 Jul 2026. (https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/kit-extension-sample-defectsgen)
- The Omniverse Launcher was deprecated on 1 Oct 2025. The migration path is Kit, the Kit App Template and GitHub/NGC downloads. (https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse/legacy-tools)
- Isaac Sim requirements (current docs): x86_64 Ubuntu 22.04/24.04 or Windows 11, or aarch64 only on DGX Spark. An RTX GPU with RT cores is required: minimum GeForce RTX 4080 16 GB; A100/H100 not supported; 32 GB RAM minimum. macOS and Apple Silicon are not listed. (https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/installation/requirements.html)
- Isaac Sim cloud options: Isaac Launchable, NVIDIA Brev, AWS, Azure, GCP and others, running as a container (headless) that streams to the desktop. Colab support was not found. (https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/6.2.0/installation/install_cloud.html)
- NVIDIA Cosmos-AnomalyGen-PCB-2B (released 06/02/2026) inpaints a defect into a masked region of a clean image (Cosmos-Predict2 2B base, 512x512 output). It was tested on A100/H100/RTX 6000 under Linux and gives no numeric evaluation. Partner material says Metal/Glass variants and a 'Defect Image Generation' skill exist. Wistron's claimed +5-20% accuracy is vendor-reported (UNVERIFIED). (https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos-AnomalyGen-PCB-2B ; https://www.globalsmt.net/?p=104113)
- Tremblay et al. (NVIDIA, CVPRW 2018): training on domain-randomised synthetic data and then fine-tuning on real KITTI data beat training on real data alone. This is the origin of NVIDIA's 'train in sim' recipe for perception, and it still uses real fine-tuning. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06516)
- Carlson et al. (ECCVW 2018): augmenting synthetic images with physically based camera effects (chromatic aberration, blur, exposure, noise, colour) narrows the synthetic-to-real gap for detection. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07721v6)
- TUHH (SPIE) borescope inspection of cast parts: rendered images adapted with CycleGAN toward real images trained detectors that generalised to real data. The paper states that synthetic samples lack properties of real images (the domain gap). (https://tore.tuhh.de/bitstreams/77fa7cd0-3361-4b53-8f6b-0e18bd6bea23/download)
- Blender Cycles has rendered on Apple Silicon GPUs through Metal since Blender 3.1 (March 2022, macOS 12.3+), so a Blender-based twin runs on the team's M3. Whether BlenderProc runs on Apple Silicon is UNVERIFIED. (https://9to5mac.com/2022/03/09/blender-3-1-update-adds-metal-gpu-rendering-for-m1-macs/)
- JarvisLabs on-demand prices from third-party listings, so confirm before use: L4 about $0.44/h, RTX A5000 about $0.49/h, RTX A6000 (48 GB, has RT cores) about $0.79/h. Whether Isaac Sim runs on these sub-spec GPUs is UNVERIFIED. (https://gpuperhour.com/providers/jarvislabs)
- Aerospace relevance of scratches: FAA airworthiness directives (for example the 2026 rule on 767-200/-300) target 'scribe lines' at fuselage skin lap and butt joints, typically caused by sealant or paint removal, because they can grow into fatigue cracks. (https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-04831.pdf)
- (calc) IMX500 with the 4.74 mm lens and a 6.287 x 4.712 mm sensor gives HFOV about 67.1 deg. At 200 mm the field is about 265 x 199 mm, about 65 um/px at 4056 px. A 640x640 tile at native resolution covers about 42 x 42 mm. A whole frame downscaled to 640 px is about 0.41 mm/px, so 0.3 mm scratches become sub-pixel unless tiled. (calc from team-supplied sensor/lens specs (IMX500 6.287x4.712 mm, f=4.74 mm, 4056x3040, 200 mm))
- Hailo-10H HEF compilation needs DFC 5.1.x on x86 Linux or Colab to match the Pi's HailoRT 5.1.1. Hailo's guidance is at least 1024 calibration samples (raw 0-255 when the .alls normalises), with a per-output SNR of at least 10 dB. (https://github.com/hailo-ai/hailo_model_zoo)

## Recommendations

- Reframe the pitch honestly. Say 'a digital twin of our inspection cell for synthetic pretraining, validated on real parts', not 'trained in simulation, works directly in the real world'. Every published metal-defect study (Fulir, ScratchSim, SynSur, BladeSynth, NVIDIA's own 2023 example) shows synthetic-only scoring below real-only, and judges will test on real plates.
- Do not use Omniverse, Isaac Sim or Cosmos for Phase 1. They need an x86 RTX machine with RT cores (not the M3; A100/H100 unsupported), the Launcher is deprecated, and the defectsgen sample is archived. Use Blender 4.x/5.x with Python (bpy) scripts and Cycles on Metal on the M3. Treat BlenderProc as optional: try it on the Mac, and if it fails, run it on a JarvisLabs Linux box. Mention Replicator/Isaac Sim only as a Phase 2 option on a rented RTX A6000/L40S-class GPU (UNVERIFIED compatibility).
- Scope the twin to what you physically have: the 100x100x3 mm plate. For aircraft framing, model a 'skin coupon': 3-4 drilled fastener holes, scribe-line scratches, punch dents, edge notches from holes, and a penetrant-developed variant. Model the IMX500 exactly (f = 4.74 mm, 6.287 x 4.712 mm sensor, about 200 mm working distance, ~65 um/px (calc)). Model both LED pods at their real positions (14 deg toe-in, UV strips tilted 16 deg) and add a room HDRI for ambient light. Do not claim it is a twin of an aircraft.
- Render at the deployment scale: 640x640 tiles at about 65 um/px (about 42 x 42 mm of plate), the same tiling the Hailo YOLO uses on the Pi. Never train on full frames downscaled to 640 px, which makes 0.3 mm defects sub-pixel (calc).
- Assign each defect class an expected difficulty and model it accordingly. EASY: punch dents (conical/spherical displacement, 0.5-2 mm diameter), drilled pits and holes (boolean), and penetrant indications (red bleed lines and dots on a grainy white developer, done as 2D compositing over real or rendered developer texture). MEDIUM: edge slits and notches (thin boolean cuts). HARD: fine scratches (a groove plus burr ridges 0.05-0.4 mm wide on an anisotropic brushed base; visibility depends on glint angle) and corrosion (procedural albedo/roughness noise; expect poor transfer). DO NOT simulate tight cracks without penetrant: they are sub-pixel at 65 um/px, so detect cracks through the penetrant-indication class.
- Domain randomisation ranges to start from (tune against 20 real reference photos): Principled BSDF roughness 0.08-0.45; anisotropy 0-0.8 with random brushing direction; normal-noise strength and scale; white LED intensity +/-40% and colour temperature 4000-6500 K; HDRI rotation and strength; working distance 170-260 mm; tilt +/-25 deg; roll 0-360 deg; handheld motion blur 0-3 px; slight defocus; Poisson-Gaussian sensor noise plus JPEG quality 70-95 (Carlson et al.). Distractors: unlabelled fingerprints, oil smudges, water spots, black nitrile glove fingers, bench backgrounds, and unlabelled micro-marks (Fulir's 'insignificant defects').
- Label by visibility, not geometry. Render masks in a separate pass. Drop or ignore any defect whose rendered contrast inside the mask is below a threshold, or which is under about 2 px wide at render scale, as ScratchSim does with its viewing-angle threshold. This avoids Fulir's over-labelling. Export YOLO labels automatically.
- Volume: 2,000-3,000 synthetic tiles is enough. SynthRender gained only +0.3 mAP going from 4k to 8k images, and BladeSynth plateaued at 50% of its data. Benchmark 20 frames first: at an assumed 2-3 s per frame on the M3 (UNVERIFIED), 2,500 frames take about 1.5-2 h (calc). Use enough samples and avoid aggressive denoising, which can erase hairline scratches (reasoning, UNVERIFIED).
- Let real data carry the claims. Spend one day capturing the 5 plates (~300-350 tiles per existing plan) with the real device in room light. Split by plate: train/fine-tune on P1-P4 and hold out P5 completely. Do not tune twin parameters against P5 images.
- Run and report a 5-row ablation with yolov8n or yolov11n (both in Hailo Model Zoo v5.1.0) on Colab or JarvisLabs: (1) real-only, (2) real-only with 25% of the real data, (3) synthetic-only, (4) synthetic pretrain then real fine-tune, (5) mixed. On P5 report per-class recall with Wilson 95% intervals, false positives per clean plate, and mAP50. This turns the digital-twin idea into a measured, defensible result whatever the outcome.
- Compile the deployed model with DFC 5.1.0 on Colab. Use real captured tiles, at least 1024 including augmentations, as the INT8 calibration set rather than renders, so quantisation statistics match what the camera actually sees (reasoning). Check per-output SNR of at least 10 dB.
- Give the QC assistant a 'simulation' it can actually use. Have the twin export structured defect records (class, size in mm, distance to the nearest fastener hole or edge, severity from rule thresholds) and use a few hundred of them to test and regression-check the Llama/ExecuTorch advice prompts or RAG for consistency and correct severity wording. Do not claim the LLM was trained in simulation.
- Gate the time spent. Cap synthetic work at about 4 person-days, from about 6 to 12 Nov. If by about 13 Nov fine-tuning from synthetic does not beat real-only on P5, keep the twin as a report section and ablation, ship the real-only model, and move effort to the mandatory Llama/ExecuTorch path, the Hailo HEF and the demo video.
- For the report and demo, show render-vs-photo pairs of the same defect under the same LED geometry, the ablation table with confidence intervals, and an explicit limitations section: synthetic data struggles with scratches and corrosion, and this is not certified NDT.

## Risks

- Synthetic-only will most likely underperform on real plates: expect a gap of about 0.15-0.45 mAP50 or about 40% relative F1 on scratch, dent and pit tasks. Demoing or claiming 'trained in sim, works in the real world' invites a visible failure in front of judges.
- Shiny aluminium causes specular glare and edge glints that synthetic-trained models flag as scratches (NVIDIA 2023 blog, Fulir). Fine scratches and corrosion transfer worst (BladeSynth, ScratchSim).
- Over-labelling: masks that include defects the camera cannot actually see train the model to hallucinate, lowering precision and recall (Fulir).
- Schedule: 18 days remain until 23 Nov. Debugging a Blender pipeline can easily take a week and starve real data capture, the Hailo compile and the mandatory Llama-via-ExecuTorch work, which judges weigh directly.
- The NVIDIA stack is a trap for this team: no macOS support, RTX GPUs with RT cores required (not A100/H100), a deprecated Launcher, an archived defect extension, and Linux-only Cosmos AnomalyGen tested on A100/H100. Cloud setup alone could take days.
- Evaluation leakage and small samples: with about 50 physical defects, confidence intervals are wide (for example 13/15 detected gives 62-96%). Tuning twin parameters against the test plate inflates results.
- Aerospace framing: the team has no real aircraft parts, real SRM damage limits are proprietary, and aerospace NDT is certification-controlled. Present the work as aircraft-style coupons and a decision-support prototype, not airworthiness inspection.
- Hailo INT8 quantisation calibrated on renders, or HEF/runtime version mismatches (DFC 5.1.x vs HailoRT 5.1.1), can silently degrade on-device accuracy compared with the Colab results.
- Several numbers are vendor- or partner-reported (Wistron +5-20%, Rolls-Royce time savings, zeroG >95% in simulation) or come from tiny test sets (ScratchSim 16 test images, BladeSynth 10 qualitative). Do not cite them as proof that sim-to-real works.
- Licensing and IP: use CC0 HDRIs and textures (Poly Haven, ambientCG) and document them. The Cosmos models fall under the NVIDIA Open Model Agreement. Keep dataset and model licence notes in DATASETS.md, as the challenge's IP clause requires.
